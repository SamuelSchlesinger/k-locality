\begin{abstract}
We study the minimum number of binary latent variables needed to realize a
distribution as the observed marginal of the topological closure of a fully
connected, bounded-order hierarchical exponential family.  The resulting
$k$-localization complexity $\LC_k(D)$ charges only latent coordinates:
interaction coefficients may be arbitrary reals, hidden variables may
interact with one another, and the lifted joint law may lie on a boundary
face.  For every fixed $k$, we determine the worst-case scale up to constants
depending only on $k$:
\[
  \max_{D\text{ on }\{0,1\}^n}\LC_k(D)=\Theta_k(2^{n/k}).
\]
The upper bound is an exposed block-feature graph preserving the complete
probability table; the lower bound is a projective marginal-variety
obstruction that includes every boundary lift.  Our principal structured
example lies in the exchangeable class: every exchangeable law has
$\LC_k(D)=O_k(n^{1/k})$, whereas the full-support target law at inverse
temperature one
\[
  D_n(x)\propto
  \exp\!\left(-2^{|x|/2^{\lfloor\log_2(n+1)\rfloor}}\right)
\]
has $\LC_k(D_n)=\Theta_k(n^{1/k})$ and likelihood ratio below $e^3$.
Thus a closed-form, uniformly conditioned law attains the worst exchangeable
scale even though the competing lifted models may use boundary faces.

The marginal obstruction yields exact polynomial certificates and effective
hard rational Gibbs rays.  A complementary theorem transfers arbitrary
degree-reduction lower bounds to full-support laws:
\[
  \liminf_{t\downarrow0}\LC_k(D_{f,t})
  \ge \operatorname{aux}_k(f).
\]
Its arbitrary-face proof uses closedness of a finite tropical image.
Secondary results give exact quasipolynomial recognition for dense rational
tables and two-way support comparisons with deterministic and
nondeterministic circuits.  These are algorithmic and barrier theorems, not
new circuit lower bounds: the structured separation above is carried by the
weights of a full-support law.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Complexity measures for distributions ask how economically a computational or
statistical model can represent a target law.  Generator complexity, for
example, charges for a circuit that transforms uniform random bits into the
target distribution \cite{vi10}.  Here the resource is different: we charge for
the number of latent binary variables needed to make the lifted distribution a
bounded-order maximum-entropy model.  Equivalently, we ask for the smallest
Boolean extension on which both the support and the relative log weights of a
table are described by interactions of order at most $k$.

Throughout, the ambient interaction hypergraph is complete and the model is
its topological closure.  We count latent bits rather than parameters or
coefficient precision, allow hidden--hidden interactions, and permit a
full-support visible law to be represented by a boundary joint law.  Thus
$\LC_2$ is not restricted-Boltzmann-machine complexity, and ``fixed
temperature'' below refers to the target visible family rather than to an
interior finite-parameter lift.  Section~\ref{sec:locality} gives the precise
definition and resource convention.

The boundary of the exponential family is essential.  A local model may have
zeros even when none of its individual marginal cells vanishes.  The correct
global object is the face of the $k$-marginal polytope containing the moment
vector: an exposing functional is a nonnegative degree-$k$ energy whose ground
space is exactly the support, while a second degree-$k$ potential controls all
positive weights on that face.  This face--Gibbs description lets us treat
hidden-variable representations uniformly across positive and boundary laws.

The same description gives a compositional reduction calculus.  A source
distribution may be used as a free base measure, after which bounded-order
Gibbs factors add fresh coordinates and the target is read from designated
coordinates.  The resulting Gibbs-reduction cost composes additively, while
the cost from the zero-variable law to $D$ is exactly
$n+\LC_k(D)$.  Facial conditioning and circuit-trace constructions are two
instances of this calculus.

Our central question is quantitative: how many hidden bits are necessary in
the worst case, and can the obstruction be witnessed by a structured law
rather than by dimension counting alone?  An exposed block-feature dictionary
gives a full-weight lift with $O_k(2^{n/k})$ hidden bits.  A projective marginal
variety, taken over every lifted support face, gives the matching generic lower
bound and exact eliminants.  The same machinery, combined with facial
conditioning and an Eisenstein--Lindemann argument, yields a closed-form
exchangeable law whose probabilities remain within a universal constant ratio
but whose localization complexity grows as $\Theta_k(n^{1/k})$.

Circuit traces provide a useful contrast rather than the source of these
separations.  Fan-in-two computations have quadratic ground-space encodings,
and every exposed ground space compiles back into a nondeterministic support
circuit.  Consequently, strong explicit lower bounds for flat supports meet a
Boolean-circuit barrier.  Full-support laws avoid that barrier because their
support recognizer is trivial and their complexity lies entirely in the
weights.

The primary theorem chain is as follows.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Sharp extension scales and a structured hard law.}
  We determine the worst-case and almost-everywhere scale as
  $\Theta_k(2^{n/k})$.  The upper construction exposes a feature graph and
  preserves the complete probability table, not merely an objective or its
  minimizers.  The lower bound uses one projective marginal variety containing
  the images of all lifted support faces.  Inside the exchangeable class we
  prove the sharp scale $\Theta_k(n^{1/k})$ and exhibit a uniformly
  conditioned closed-form target at inverse temperature one attaining it
  (Theorems~\ref{thm:universal-lift} and~\ref{thm:generic-positive},
  Corollary~\ref{cor:worst-case}, and
  Theorem~\ref{thm:exchangeable-window}).
  \item \emph{Algebraic certificates and tropical transfer.}
  The marginal ideal supplies exact hidden-bit lower-bound certificates and
  computable hard rational Gibbs rays with effective cold intervals.  The
  zero-temperature theorem imports any unrestricted
  degree-$k$ auxiliary-variable lower bound into full-support localization.
  Its proof works for arbitrary real energies uniformly across boundary faces.
  It also yields noisy parity and optimal-scale closed-form rays
  (Theorems~\ref{thm:algebraic-certificate} and~\ref{thm:zero-temperature},
  Corollaries~\ref{cor:effective-tables}, \ref{cor:noisy-parity},
  and~\ref{cor:superincreasing}).
\end{itemize}
Two groups of consequences locate the measure algorithmically and
complexity-theoretically.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Exact dense-table recognition.}
  For dense exact rational tables, face enumeration gives deterministic
  quasipolynomial recognition for every linear latent budget, with a sharper
  exponent for fixed-budget full-support tables; the zero-latent case is in
  polynomial time.  Under ETH, none of these dense restrictions is NP-hard
  under polynomial-output reductions
  (Theorems~\ref{thm:qp-recognition} and~\ref{thm:full-support-recognition},
  Proposition~\ref{prop:zero-latent-p},
  Corollary~\ref{cor:recognition-eth}).
  \item \emph{The support and circuit boundary.}
  Quadratic NAND synthesis maps circuit traces to localizations, while exposed
  faces compile back into nondeterministic support circuits.  This gives a
  static two-way simulation and a circuit barrier, not a new circuit lower
  bound (Theorems~\ref{thm:nand-synthesis} and~\ref{thm:converse}); a
  Razborov--Rudich argument makes the barrier precise
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:natural-proofs}).  An appendix develops finer
  support invariants, almost-all-supports bounds, exact parity localization,
  and adjacent hierarchy separations (Appendix~\ref{app:support-invariants}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Complexity-theoretic status.}
The paper does not prove a new lower bound for a standard circuit class.  Its
circuit statements are simulations and barriers: they identify what a strong
explicit flat-support localization bound would imply.  The unconditional
structured lower bound is instead weight-sensitive, has full support, and uses
transcendental probabilities despite its succinct closed form.  The dense
rational recognition algorithms concern a separate explicit-table input
model.  We keep these three regimes distinct throughout.

\section{Locality and localization}\label{sec:locality}

Throughout, $n\ge1$.  Fix a distribution $D$ on $\bits^n$, with mass function
$\rho$, and write $S=\supp(D)=\{x:\rho(x)>0\}$.  For every nonempty finite set
$T$, let $U_T$ denote the uniform distribution on $T$.  We identify every
function on a Boolean cube with its unique canonical multilinear
representative, obtained modulo the relations $x_i^2=x_i$; polynomial degree
always refers to this representative.

\begin{definition}[Marginal model]
A distribution $Q$ on $(X,H)\in\bits^n\times\bits^\ell$ is a marginal model of
$D$ if
\[
  \sum_{h\in\bits^\ell}Q(x,h)=\rho(x)
  \quad\text{for every }x\in\bits^n.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[$k$-local distribution]
A distribution $Q$ on $\bits^N$ is $k$-local if there is a finite collection of
scopes $A_i\subseteq[N]$, $|A_i|\le k$, such that $Q$ maximizes Shannon entropy
among all distributions having the same $A_i$-marginals as $Q$.
\end{definition}

Entropy is strictly concave, so the maximizer in this definition is unique.
The constraints may be taken to be entries of marginal tables or,
equivalently for binary variables, expectations of multilinear monomials.

\begin{definition}[$k$-localization complexity]
A $k$-local marginal model of $D$ is a $k$-localization of $D$.  For $k\ge2$,
\[
  \LC_k(D)=\min\{\ell:D\text{ has a $k$-localization with $\ell$ latent bits}\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Resource convention.}
For $k=2$, this is hidden-unit complexity in the topological closure of the
general binary Boltzmann-machine model: quadratic energies allow arbitrary
biases and all visible--visible, visible--hidden, and hidden--hidden couplings.
It is therefore not restricted-Boltzmann-machine complexity.  More generally,
this is a nonuniform extension-dimension measure: it charges latent bits, not
the number or bit precision of interaction coefficients, and it permits
arbitrary real parameters and boundary limits.  This convention makes the
comparison with exact finite-description circuits intentionally asymmetric;
for example, a uniform-seed exact generator does not exist for a nondyadic
law.  The rational certificates below show that large localization complexity
is not confined to transcendental tables, but coefficient-bounded and
approximate versions are distinct measures and remain open.

\begin{proposition}[Universal existence]\label{prop:existence}
Every distribution $D$ has a $2$-localization with at most $|S|$ latent bits.
Consequently $\LC_k(D)$ is finite for every $k\ge2$ and
\[
  \LC_k(D)\le \LC_2(D)\le |S|.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Introduce one bit $H_x$ for each $x\in S$.  Let $Q$ put mass $\rho(x)$ on the
single lifted state in which $X=x$, $H_x=1$, and $H_{x'}=0$ for $x'\ne x$.
Constrain every pair marginal $(H_x,X_i)$ to equal its marginal under $Q$.

Any feasible distribution $R$ has $\Pr_R(H_x=1)=\rho(x)$, and $H_x=1$ forces
$X=x$.  The events $\{H_x=1\}$ are pairwise disjoint: two distinct support
points differ in some visible coordinate, which the corresponding active bits
would force to both values.  Their probabilities sum to one, so exactly one is
active almost surely.  It follows that $R=Q$.  Thus $Q$ is the unique feasible
distribution and hence the maximum-entropy feasible distribution.  Monotonicity
in $k$ is immediate.
\end{proof}

This construction is deliberately crude.  The measure is interesting when
structure in $D$ permits far fewer latent bits.

\section{Marginal polytopes and ground states}\label{sec:polytopes}

For $A\subseteq[N]$, let $y_A=\prod_{i\in A}y_i$, with $y_\varnothing=1$.
Define the order-$k$ feature map
\[
  \chi_k:\bits^N\longrightarrow\bits^{d_k(N)},
  \qquad
  \chi_k(y)=(y_A)_{A\subseteq[N],\ |A|\le k},
  \qquad
  d_k(N)=\sum_{j=0}^k\binom Nj,
\]
and the $k$-marginal polytope
\[
  P_k(N)=\operatorname{conv}\{\chi_k(y):y\in\bits^N\}.
\]
The vector $\E_Q[\chi_k]$ is equivalent to the full collection of marginals on
at most $k$ variables.  One direction is immediate from
$\E[Y_A]=\Pr(Y_i=1\text{ for all }i\in A)$; the other follows by expanding
products of $Y_i$ and $1-Y_i$.

\begin{lemma}[Canonical constraints]\label{lem:canonical}
A distribution $Q$ is $k$-local if and only if it maximizes entropy among all
distributions $R$ satisfying
\[
  \E_R[\chi_k]=\E_Q[\chi_k].
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The reverse implication is the definition, using all scopes of size at most
$k$.  For the forward implication, every distribution matching all
order-at-most-$k$ marginals also matches the particular witnessing marginals.
Adding these constraints preserves the maximality of $Q$.
\end{proof}

We use one elementary support fact about entropy.

\begin{lemma}[Maximal feasible support]\label{lem:max-support}
Let $\mathcal F$ be a nonempty convex subset of a finite probability simplex,
defined by affine equalities.  If $Q$ maximizes entropy on $\mathcal F$, then
\[
  \supp(Q)=\bigcup_{R\in\mathcal F}\supp(R).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The inclusion from left to right is trivial.  If $R(y)>0$ but $Q(y)=0$, then
$Q_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)Q+\varepsilon R$ is feasible.  The new coordinate
contributes $-\varepsilon R(y)\log(\varepsilon R(y))$ to entropy, whereas the
total first-order change from coordinates already in $\supp(Q)$ is $O(\varepsilon)$.
For sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$, the $-\varepsilon\log\varepsilon$
term dominates and $H(Q_\varepsilon)>H(Q)$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Face--Gibbs characterization]\label{thm:face-gibbs}
For a distribution $Q$ on $\bits^N$, the following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $Q$ is $k$-local.
  \item There are a face $F$ of $P_k(N)$ and a vector
  $\theta\in\mathbb R^{d_k(N)}$ such that, with
  $T=\chi_k^{-1}(F)$,
  \[
    \supp(Q)=T,
    \qquad
    Q(y)=\frac{\exp(\langle\theta,\chi_k(y)\rangle)}
    {\sum_{z\in T}\exp(\langle\theta,\chi_k(z)\rangle)}
    \quad (y\in T).
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume first that $Q$ is $k$-local and put
$b=\E_Q[\chi_k]\in P_k(N)$.  Let $F$ be the unique minimal face of $P_k(N)$
containing $b$; then $b$ lies in the relative interior of $F$.  A convex
representation of $b$ can use only feature vectors in $F$.  Conversely, for
every $v=\chi_k(y)\in F$, relative interiority lets us write
$b=\varepsilon v+(1-\varepsilon)b'$ for some $\varepsilon>0$ and $b'\in F$,
so $y$ occurs with positive mass in some distribution having moment vector
$b$.  Lemmas~\ref{lem:canonical} and \ref{lem:max-support} therefore give
\[
  \supp(Q)=\{y:\chi_k(y)\in F\}.
\]

On this support $Q$ is strictly positive.  Let $A_T$ be the matrix whose
columns are the feature vectors $\chi_k(y)$ for $y\in T$.  A feasible tangent
direction is any $u\in\ker A_T$; the constant feature already enforces
$\sum_y u_y=0$.  First-order optimality of entropy at the interior point
$Q|_T$ gives
\[
  \langle -1-\log Q,u\rangle=0
  \qquad(u\in\ker A_T).
\]
Hence $\log Q$ lies in the row space of $A_T$, with the constant feature
absorbing the affine term.  This is exactly the displayed Gibbs form.

Conversely, suppose $Q$ has the displayed form.  Choose an affine functional
$a$ exposing $F$, oriented so that
\[
  E(y):=a(\chi_k(y))\ge0,
  \qquad E(y)=0\Longleftrightarrow y\in T.
\]
If $R$ has the same order-$k$ moments as $Q$, then
$\E_R[E]=\E_Q[E]=0$.  Hence $R$ is supported on $T$.  Moreover
$\E_R[\log Q]=\E_Q[\log Q]$, since $\log Q$ is linear in $\chi_k$ on $T$.
Evaluating relative entropy on the common support $T$, nonnegativity of
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(R\|Q)$ gives
\[
  0\le D_{\mathrm{KL}}(R\|Q)
  =-H(R)-\E_R[\log Q]
  =-H(R)+H(Q).
\]
Thus $Q$ is the maximum-entropy distribution with its order-$k$ moments, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:canonical} makes it $k$-local.
\end{proof}

This theorem is a specialization of the description of the closure of a finite
exponential family by faces of its marginal polytope
\cite{rauh11,malago11}.  We record the form needed below.

\begin{corollary}[Ground-state support]\label{cor:ground-state}
If $Q$ is $k$-local, there is a multilinear polynomial
\[
  E(y)=\sum_{A\subseteq[N],\ |A|\le k}a_A y_A
\]
such that $E(y)\ge0$ on $\bits^N$ and
\[
  \supp(Q)=\{y:E(y)=0\}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use an affine functional exposing the face in
Theorem~\ref{thm:face-gibbs} and compose it with $\chi_k$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Relative localization and Gibbs reductions}
\label{sec:gibbs-reductions}

The face--Gibbs form also gives a notion of reduction between distributions.
The source law contributes its entire probability table as a base measure;
only coordinates introduced after that source are charged.

\begin{definition}[$k$-Gibbs reduction]
\label{def:gibbs-reduction}
Let $P$ be a distribution on $\bits^I$ and let $D$ be a distribution on
$\bits^J$, where $I$ and $J$ are finite coordinate sets.  A relative
$k$-Gibbs extension of $P$ consists of a finite fresh coordinate set $A$
disjoint from $I$, multilinear polynomials $E,\theta$ on
$\bits^{I\sqcup A}$ of degree at most $k$, and the distribution
\[
  R(y,a)=\frac{1}{Z}
  P(y)\,\mathbf 1_{\{E(y,a)=0\}}\exp(\theta(y,a)),
  \tag{GR}\label{eq:gibbs-reduction}
\]
where $E\ge0$ on the Boolean cube and the normalizing constant $Z$ is
positive.  A $k$-Gibbs reduction from $P$ to $D$ additionally specifies an
injection $\iota:J\hookrightarrow I\sqcup A$ such that $D$ is the pushforward
of $R$ under the coordinate projection
$\pi_\iota(z)_j=z_{\iota(j)}$.

The cost of the reduction is $|A|$, including both fresh output coordinates
and fresh coordinates that are marginalized out.  We write
\[
  \GRed_k(P\mathbin{\to}D)
\]
for the minimum cost.  Source coordinates are never charged, even when they
are not designated as target coordinates and therefore act as latent
variables for $D$.
\end{definition}

In the common disjoint-output form, $P$ is on $m$ source bits, the $n$ target
bits $X$ and $\ell$ workspace bits $H$ are fresh, and
\eqref{eq:gibbs-reduction} says
\[
  D(x)=\frac{1}{Z}
  \sum_{y\in\bits^m}\sum_{h\in\bits^\ell}
  P(y)\,\mathbf 1_{\{E(y,x,h)=0\}}\exp(\theta(y,x,h)).
  \tag{GR$'$}\label{eq:gibbs-reduction-disjoint}
\]
This reduction has cost $n+\ell$; its hidden overhead relative to the free
source is $\ell$.

\begin{remark}[Weight access, not sample access]
The source marginal of $R$ in \eqref{eq:gibbs-reduction} need not equal $P$:
the local Gibbs factor
may reweight source states.  Thus this is a projective reduction with access
to the weights of $P$, not a claim that one sample from $P$ is transformed by
a stochastic channel.  It inherits the nonuniform convention of $\LC_k$;
uniform reductions, bounded coefficient descriptions, oracle copies, and
approximate sample conversion are separate refinements.
\end{remark}

Let $\mathbf 1$ denote the unique distribution on the zero-coordinate cube.
The fresh-coordinate convention makes Gibbs reductions compositional and
recovers localization complexity as the absolute special case.

\begin{proposition}[Gibbs-reduction calculus]
\label{prop:gibbs-reduction-calculus}
For $k\ge2$ and finite Boolean distributions $P,D,R$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\GRed_k(P\mathbin{\to}P)=0$;
  \item
  \[
    \GRed_k(P\mathbin{\to}R)
    \le
    \GRed_k(P\mathbin{\to}D)
    +\GRed_k(D\mathbin{\to}R);
    \tag{Comp}\label{eq:gibbs-composition}
  \]
  \item if $D$ is on $n$ bits, then
  \[
    \GRed_k(\mathbf 1\mathbin{\to}D)=n+\LC_k(D);
    \tag{Abs}\label{eq:gibbs-absolute}
  \]
  \item reductions tensor in parallel:
  \[
    \GRed_k(P_1\otimes P_2\mathbin{\to}D_1\otimes D_2)
    \le
    \GRed_k(P_1\mathbin{\to}D_1)
    +\GRed_k(P_2\mathbin{\to}D_2).
    \tag{Ten}\label{eq:gibbs-tensor}
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the identity, introduce no fresh coordinates, take $E=\theta=0$, and
designate every source coordinate as an output coordinate.

For composition, make the fresh coordinate sets $A$ and $B$ of the two
reductions disjoint.  Substitute the designated output coordinates of the
first reduction for the source coordinates of the second.  If their Gibbs
data are $(E_1,\theta_1)$ and $(E_2,\theta_2)$, use
\[
  E=E_1+E_2,
  \qquad
  \theta=\theta_1+\theta_2
\]
after this coordinate substitution.  Both summands of $E$ are nonnegative,
so its zero set is the intersection of the two relative support conditions;
degree does not increase.  Marginalizing the first extension over each of its
designated output fibers produces $D$, after which the second Gibbs factor
produces $R$.  Equivalently, this is associativity of the two finite sums in
\eqref{eq:gibbs-reduction-disjoint}.  The composite introduces exactly the
coordinates in $A\sqcup B$, which proves
\eqref{eq:gibbs-composition}.

For \eqref{eq:gibbs-absolute}, a reduction from $\mathbf 1$ of cost $r$ is an
ordinary $k$-local distribution on its $r$ fresh coordinates by
Theorem~\ref{thm:face-gibbs}.  Its $n$ designated coordinates have marginal
$D$, so the other $r-n$ coordinates give a $k$-localization and
$n+\LC_k(D)\le r$.  Conversely, apply the face--Gibbs form to an optimal
$k$-localization of $D$ and introduce its $n+\LC_k(D)$ total coordinates as
fresh coordinates.  This gives the reverse inequality.  Finally, placing two
reductions on disjoint coordinates and adding their exposing energies and
potentials proves \eqref{eq:gibbs-tensor}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Compiling a source distribution]
\label{cor:gibbs-reduction-compilation}
If $P$ is on $m$ bits and $D$ is on $n$ bits, then
\[
  n+\LC_k(D)
  \le
  m+\LC_k(P)+\GRed_k(P\mathbin{\to}D).
  \tag{Sub}\label{eq:gibbs-compilation}
\]
In particular, a disjoint-output reduction of the form
\eqref{eq:gibbs-reduction-disjoint} with $\ell$
workspace bits gives
\[
  \LC_k(D)\le m+\LC_k(P)+\ell.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Compose an optimal reduction from $\mathbf 1$ to $P$ with an optimal reduction
from $P$ to $D$, then apply \eqref{eq:gibbs-composition} and
\eqref{eq:gibbs-absolute}.
\end{proof}

Two operations used later are already Gibbs reductions.  If $C$ is exposed by
a nonnegative degree-at-most-$k$ polynomial and $P(C)>0$, multiplying $P$ by
$\mathbf 1_C$ and renormalizing gives
\[
  \GRed_k(P\mathbin{\to}P(\mathord\cdot\mid C))=0.
\]
Thus Lemma~\ref{lem:facial-conditioning} is monotonicity under a zero-cost
reduction on the same coordinates.  Likewise, a circuit generator is a Gibbs
reduction from its uniform seed law: the fresh trace and output-copy bits are
exposed by the sum of the gate and equality penalties.  Compiling the uniform
source via Corollary~\ref{cor:gibbs-reduction-compilation} recovers the bounds
in Theorem~\ref{thm:upper-bounds} and its quadratic NAND specialization.

\subsection{A boundary caution}

Consider three bits $X,Y,Z$ with pairwise marginals
\[
\Pr(X,Y)=\frac1{20}\begin{pmatrix}9&1\\1&9\end{pmatrix},\quad
\Pr(X,Z)=\frac1{10}\begin{pmatrix}4&1\\1&4\end{pmatrix},\quad
\Pr(Y,Z)=\frac1{20}\begin{pmatrix}7&3\\3&7\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Every local cell is positive, but the feasible joint displayed below gives
zero mass to $011$ and $100$.  This refutes the claim that local positivity
implies a strictly positive feasible joint.

For completeness, in lexicographic order $000,001,\ldots,111$, the joint
probability vector is
\[
  \frac1{20}(7,2,1,0,0,1,2,7).
\]
Direct summation gives the three displayed marginals.  Uniqueness is not needed
for the obstruction below.

The corrected certificate is already quadratic:
\[
  E(x,y,z)=x-xy-xz+yz=(x-y)(x-z).
\]
On the Boolean cube, $E$ equals one at $011$ and $100$ and zero elsewhere.
The prescribed marginals imply
\[
  \E[E(X,Y,Z)]
  =\frac12-\frac9{20}-\frac25+\frac7{20}=0.
\]
Since $E\ge0$, every feasible joint is supported on $E^{-1}(0)$.  Thus no
single marginal entry rules out either state; a weighted sum of pairwise
statistics exposes the global support face.

\section{Universal feature lifts}\label{sec:universal-lifts}

Universal sets for quadratization show that every pseudo-Boolean objective can
be reduced to degree two with $O(2^{n/2})$ auxiliary variables
\cite{anthony17}.  A related feature construction gives an exact
distributional lift and extends to every target locality $k$.

At the sparse extreme, neighborliness of marginal polytopes already eliminates
the need for hidden variables.

\begin{proposition}[Sparse distributions need no lift]
\label{prop:sparse-no-lift}
If $D$ is a distribution on $\bits^n$ and
$|\supp(D)|\le2^k-1$, then $\LC_k(D)=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
When $k\ge n$, the full joint marginal uniquely determines $D$.  When $k<n$,
the smallest non-face of the complete order-$k$ interaction complex has size
$k+1$.  Kahle's neighborliness theorem says that every distribution supported
on fewer than $2^k$ atoms belongs to the closure of this hierarchical model
\cite[Theorem~14]{kahle10}.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:face-gibbs}, this closure is
exactly the set of $k$-local distributions.
\end{proof}

Let $n=qk+r$, where $0\le r<k$, and define
\[
  b_k(n)
  =r2^{q+1}+(k-r)2^q-n-k.
\]
Equivalently, partition $[n]$ into $k$ blocks $V_1,\ldots,V_k$ whose sizes
differ by at most one, allowing empty blocks when $k>n$; then
\[
  b_k(n)=\sum_{j=1}^k\bigl(2^{|V_j|}-|V_j|-1\bigr),
\]
the number of within-block subsets of size at least two.

\begin{theorem}[Universal block-feature lift]
\label{thm:universal-lift}
For every distribution $D$ on $\bits^n$ and every $k\ge2$,
\[
  \LC_k(D)\le \min\{|\supp(D)|,b_k(n)\}.
\]
In particular, for fixed $k$ every distribution has
\[
  \LC_k(D)=O_k(2^{n/k}).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The support-size bound is Proposition~\ref{prop:existence}.  We prove the
block-feature bound.  If $k\ge n$, every distribution is already $k$-local
and $b_k(n)=0$, so assume $2\le k<n$.

\emph{Step 1: expose the block-feature graph.}
For every $A\subseteq V_j$ with $|A|\ge2$, introduce a latent bit $h_A$ intended
to equal $x_A=\prod_{i\in A}x_i$.  Write $z_A=h_A$ for such a set,
$z_{\{i\}}=x_i$, and $z_\varnothing=1$.  For each nonsingleton $A$, choose
$i_A\in A$ and, in increasing order of $|A|$, impose
\[
  h_A=z_{A\setminus\{i_A\}}x_{i_A}.
\]
The quadratic Rosenberg penalty
\cite{rosenberg75}\cite[Observation~1]{boroshammer02}
\[
  R(a,b,c)=ab-2ac-2bc+3c
\]
is nonnegative on $\bits^3$ and vanishes exactly when $c=ab$.  Hence the sum
$P(x,h)$ of these penalties is nonnegative and has zero set exactly the graph
\[
  \Gamma=\{(x,h):h_A=x_A\text{ for every introduced }A\}.
\]
This follows inductively in increasing order of $|A|$.

\emph{Step 2: reduce visible degree on the graph.}
Now let $f:\bits^n\to\mathbb R$ be arbitrary and write its multilinear
expansion as $f(x)=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}c_S x_S$.  On $\Gamma$,
\[
  x_S=\prod_{j:S\cap V_j\ne\varnothing}z_{S\cap V_j}.
\]
The product has one factor for each nonempty block intersection and therefore
at most $k$ factors.  Replacing every $x_S$ by the expression on the right
gives a polynomial $\widetilde f(x,h)$ of degree at most $k$ whose restriction
to $\Gamma$ is $f$.

\emph{Step 3: expose the lifted support.}
Let $S=\supp(D)$.  Apply this construction first to the Boolean function
$u(x)=1_{\bits^n\setminus S}(x)$.  Since $P$ is positive off $\Gamma$, choose
\[
  M>\max_{(x,h)\notin\Gamma}
  \max\left\{0,\frac{-\widetilde u(x,h)}{P(x,h)}\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
  E(x,h)=\widetilde u(x,h)+M P(x,h)
\]
is nonnegative everywhere and strictly positive off $\Gamma$.  On $\Gamma$
it equals $u(x)$, so its zero set is precisely
\[
  T=\{(x,h)\in\Gamma:x\in S\}.
\]
The energy $E$ has degree at most $k$.

\emph{Step 4: lift the weights.}
Finally extend $x\mapsto\log D(x)$ arbitrarily from $S$ to the full visible
cube and call the resulting function $f$.  Define $Q$ on the lifted cube by
placing mass $D(x)$ at the unique point of $T$ above $x$ and zero elsewhere.
On $T$ its log-density is the restriction of the degree-at-most-$k$ polynomial
$\widetilde f$.  Its support is the zero set of the nonnegative
degree-at-most-$k$ energy $E$.  Because $E$ is an affine functional of
$\chi_k(x,h)$, its nonnegative zero set is the inverse image of the face it
exposes.  Theorem~\ref{thm:face-gibbs} therefore makes $Q$ $k$-local, and its
visible marginal is $D$.  The number of latent bits is $b_k(n)$.
\end{proof}

of both support and weight data.

The balanced block dictionary is the classical $k$-generator of the Boolean
lattice: every subset is the disjoint union of at most one member from each
block's power set.  Its full generator size is $b_k(n)+n$, because the visible
singletons are not charged as latent features.  Ellis proves the lower bound
$k2^{n/k}(1-o(1))$ for fixed $k$ \cite[Theorem~5]{ellis09}.  This matches the
balanced generator asymptotically when $k$ divides $n$; for other residue
classes the balanced constant is larger by
$(1+r/k)2^{-r/k}$, a factor at most about $1.061$, and exact optimality remains
open.  For $k=2$, this is the $2$-basis adapted by
Anthony, Boros, Crama, and Gruber for their universal quadratization bound
\cite[Theorems~4--5 and Remark~2]{anthony17}.  Their matching generic lower
bound for quadratization uses a related finite-union dimension argument
\cite[Theorem~1]{anthony17}.  Their stated upper-bound count includes singleton
features; here those are the original visible variables, saving $n$ latent
bits.  The proof above also uses more than preservation of an objective's
minimum: it exposes the graph of the feature map and lifts the entire
probability table.  This is why both support and relative Gibbs weights are
preserved.  The balanced dictionary and its objective-reduction use are
classical; the claimed step here is an exact probability-table strengthening
of that universal-feature construction, obtained by exposing its graph.  The
architecture allows hidden--hidden interactions and boundary joint
distributions, so its $O(2^{n/2})$ quadratic count does not contradict larger
universality bounds for restricted Boltzmann machines
\cite{montufaray11,montufarmorton15drbm}.

\section{Algebraic lower-bound certificates}
\label{sec:algebraic-certificates}

The dimension obstruction can be upgraded from a generic statement to an
exact certificate.  Put $N=n+\ell$.  In projective coordinates
$[p_x]_{x\in\bits^n}$, let $\mathcal V_{n,k,\ell}$ be the Zariski closure of
the parameterized set
\[
  p_x
  =\sum_{h\in\bits^\ell}
    \prod_{\substack{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq[N]\\|A|\le k}}
      t_A^{\prod_{i\in A}(x,h)_i}.
\]
The parameters and closure here are complex; positive real parameters recover
the visible marginals of positive order-$k$ Gibbs models.  This is the standard
toric parameterization of a discrete graphical model followed by a linear
marginal map \cite{geiger06}.

\begin{theorem}[Marginal-ideal certificate]
\label{thm:algebraic-certificate}
If $\LC_k(D)\le\ell$, then $[D]\in\mathcal V_{n,k,\ell}$.  Consequently, if a
homogeneous polynomial $F$ in the vanishing ideal
$I(\mathcal V_{n,k,\ell})$ satisfies $F(D)\ne0$, then
\[
  \LC_k(D)>\ell.
\]
The ideal is computable by eliminating the parameters $t_A$ from the displayed
map.  Moreover,
\[
  \dim\mathcal V_{n,k,\ell}\le d_k(n+\ell)-1.
\]
Thus whenever $d_k(n+\ell)<2^n$, the ideal contains a nonzero homogeneous
polynomial with integer coefficients.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A positive $k$-local joint law has, up to normalization, exactly the monomial
parameterization inside the sum.  Every boundary $k$-local law is a limit of
positive ones: if $E\ge0$ exposes its facial support $T$ and $\theta$ is its
face--Gibbs potential, then
\[
  Q_\lambda(y)\ \propto\
  \exp\!\bigl(\langle\theta,\chi_k(y)\rangle-\lambda E(y)\bigr)
\]
has full support and converges to the boundary law as $\lambda\to\infty$.
If a localization uses $m<\ell$ bits, pad it with
$\ell-m$ bits fixed to zero: adding the unary energy equal to the sum of the
new bits exposes this padded face, so the law remains $k$-local.  Thus
marginalization and closure place its visible probability vector in
$\mathcal V_{n,k,\ell}$.  This proves the certificate implication.

Writing the displayed coordinate polynomial as $q_x(\mathbf t)$, introduce a
scale variable $s$ and form, over $\mathbb Q$, the elimination ideal
\[
  \big\langle p_x-sq_x(\mathbf t):x\in\bits^n\big\rangle
  \cap\mathbb Q[p_x:x\in\bits^n].
\]
Gr\"obner elimination computes this homogeneous ideal, and clearing
denominators gives integer coefficients.  No saturation by
$\prod_A t_A$ is needed: the complex parameter torus is Zariski dense in its
affine closure, a polynomial map sends the closure of a dense set into the
closure of its image, and the scale variable forms the affine cone over the
projective image.  The parameter torus
has at most $d_k(N)-1$ nonconstant coordinates, which proves the dimension
bound.  A proper projective variety defined over $\mathbb Z$ has a nonzero
homogeneous integer polynomial in its ideal.
\end{proof}

The certificate degree can be made explicit without computing the elimination
ideal.

\begin{proposition}[Explicit certificate degree]\label{prop:certificate-degree}
Put $d=d_k(n+\ell)$ and let $\Delta\ge1$ satisfy
\[
  \binom{\Delta+2^n-1}{2^n-1}>\binom{\Delta(d-1)+d-1}{d-1}.
\]
Then $I(\mathcal V_{n,k,\ell})$ contains a nonzero homogeneous integer
polynomial of degree $\Delta$, computable by exact rational linear algebra in
the $\binom{\Delta+2^n-1}{2^n-1}$ coefficients of a general degree-$\Delta$
form.  In particular, if $d_k(n+\ell)\le2^{n-1}$, then $\Delta=2^{2n+2}$
suffices.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each parameterized coordinate
$q_x(\mathbf t)=\sum_h\prod_{A:(x,h)_A=1}t_A$ is a polynomial of degree at
most $d-1$ in the $d-1$ parameters, so substitution
$F\mapsto F((q_x)_x)$ is a linear map from degree-$\Delta$ forms in the $2^n$
coordinates to polynomials of degree at most $\Delta(d-1)$ in $d-1$
variables.  The displayed inequality makes the source dimension exceed the
target dimension, so the kernel contains a nonzero rational point; clearing
denominators gives an integer form $F$ with $F\circ q\equiv0$.  Such an $F$
vanishes on every parameterized point, hence on the projective closure
$\mathcal V_{n,k,\ell}$.

For the last claim put $m=2^n-1$, $r=d-1$, and $\Delta=2^{2n+2}$; then
$r\le2^{n-1}-1$ gives $m\ge2r$, and
\[
  \binom{\Delta+m}{m}\ge\left(\frac{\Delta+m}{m}\right)^{m}
  \ge\left(\left(\frac{\Delta}{m}\right)^{2}\right)^{r},
  \qquad
  \binom{\Delta r+r}{r}\le\bigl(e(\Delta+1)\bigr)^{r},
\]
while
$(\Delta/m)^2>(\Delta/2^n)^2=2^{2n+4}=4\cdot2^{2n+2}>e(\Delta+1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Cost of the certificate]\label{rem:certificate-cost}
Gr\"obner elimination computes the entire elimination ideal, but its
worst-case degrees are doubly exponential in general \cite{mayrmeyer82}.
Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate-degree} instead isolates one certificate:
below the halved dimension threshold its degree is singly exponential in $n$,
although the ambient linear algebra still has doubly exponential size, and
Hadamard bounds on a kernel minor bound the certificate's coefficient bit
length by the same order.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[The first marginal-ideal certificate]
For $(n,k,\ell)=(3,2,0)$, eliminating the pairwise Gibbs parameters gives the
familiar no-three-factor-interaction binomial
\[
  F(p)=p_{111}p_{100}p_{010}p_{001}
       -p_{110}p_{101}p_{011}p_{000}.
\]
Let $D$ be the rational full-support table obtained by assigning unnormalized
weight $2$ to $111$ and weight $1$ to every other string.  Then
$F(D)=9^{-4}\ne0$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:algebraic-certificate} certifies
$\LC_2(D)>0$.  This small equation is standard toric implicitization; its role
here is to illustrate exactly how an eliminant is evaluated as a localization
certificate.  For latent models the same algorithm remains exact, although
the degrees and the elimination cost can grow rapidly.
\end{example}

\begin{corollary}[Effective rational hard tables and Gibbs rays]
\label{cor:effective-tables}
If $d_k(n+\ell)<2^n$, one can effectively construct an integer-valued function
$f:\bits^n\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ and a rational number $\tau>0$ such that
\[
  \LC_k(D_{f,t})>\ell
  \qquad\text{for every real }0<t<\tau.
\]
In particular, every rational $t$ in this interval gives a rational
full-support hard table.  Consequently, for every fixed $k$ there is a
computable family of integer energies $(f_n)$, with computable rational cold
thresholds, satisfying
\[
  \LC_k(D_{f_n,t})=\Omega_k(2^{n/k})
  \qquad(0<t<\tau_n).
\]
Together with Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-lift}, this matches the worst-case
scale up to constants depending only on $k$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof roadmap]
Use Theorem~\ref{thm:algebraic-certificate} to compute a nonzero homogeneous
eliminant $F$.  Under a base-coded substitution, distinct monomials of $F$
acquire distinct exponents, so the resulting univariate polynomial is
nonzero.  Factoring out its smallest power of $t$ and bounding the remaining
integer coefficients gives an explicit rational interval on which it cannot
vanish.
Appendix~\ref{app:effective-certificates} gives the construction and the
asymptotic parameter choice; Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate-degree} bounds
the degree of $F$, and hence every constant, explicitly in the regime
$d_k(n+\ell)\le2^{n-1}$.
\end{proof}

The invariant is the classical marginal ideal itself: unlike a raw tensor
flattening, it retains all order-$k$ interactions and remains valid for joint
distributions on boundary faces.  What is specific here is its indexing by a
latent-bit budget and the effective base-coded ray that escapes the resulting
variety.  The family is highly artificial; the point is that the algebraic
obstruction is certifying rather than merely measure-theoretic.

\section{Full-support distributions}\label{sec:full-support}

Support lower bounds disappear when $D$ is strictly positive on the entire
Boolean cube, but the probabilities retain high-order interaction information.
This gives a complementary regime in which localization complexity is not a
proxy for support-circuit complexity.

Dimension gives much larger lower bounds for generic probability tables.

\begin{theorem}[Generic full-support lower bound]
\label{thm:generic-positive}
Fix $k\ge2$.  For Lebesgue-almost every full-support distribution $D$ on
$\bits^n$, every $k$-localization with $\ell$ latent bits satisfies
\[
  d_k(n+\ell)\ge2^n.
\]
Consequently there is a constant $c_k>0$ such that almost every such $D$
satisfies
\[
  \LC_k(D)+n\ge c_k2^{n/k}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each $\ell$ satisfying $d_k(n+\ell)<2^n$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:algebraic-certificate} places every visible marginal of an
$\ell$-bit localization in the proper algebraic variety
$\mathcal V_{n,k,\ell}$.  Its real points have Lebesgue measure zero in the
$(2^n-1)$-dimensional probability simplex.  There are only finitely many
$\ell$ satisfying the displayed inequality, so the union of these exceptional
sets is still null.  Thus almost every $D$ obeys
$d_k(n+\LC_k(D))\ge2^n$.  The quantitative conclusion follows from
$d_k(N)=O_k(N^k)$.  For fixed $k$, one has $n=o(2^{n/k})$, so after
decreasing the constant this also gives
$\LC_k(D)=\Omega_k(2^{n/k})$ for almost every $D$.
\end{proof}

Combining the dimension obstruction with the universal feature lift determines
the scale of the measure.

\begin{corollary}[Worst-case localization complexity]
\label{cor:worst-case}
For every fixed $k\ge2$,
\[
  \max_{D\text{ on }\bits^n}\LC_k(D)=\Theta_k(2^{n/k}).
\]
Moreover, Lebesgue-almost every full-support distribution on $\bits^n$ has
$k$-localization complexity $\Theta_k(2^{n/k})$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The upper bound for every $D$ is Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-lift}.  The matching
lower bound for almost every full-support $D$ is
Theorem~\ref{thm:generic-positive}; in particular, such distributions witness
the worst-case lower bound.
\end{proof}

This argument includes localizations whose joint distribution lies on a
proper support face; only the visible marginal is assumed to have full support.
It therefore cannot be reduced to dimension counting for positive restricted
Boltzmann machines alone.  The result is generic, while
Corollary~\ref{cor:superincreasing} gives a closed-form but extremely
ill-conditioned worst-case ray and Corollary~\ref{cor:noisy-parity} gives a
natural logarithmic example.  Theorem~\ref{thm:exchangeable-window} supplies
the missing superlogarithmic structured example: at fixed inverse temperature
one, its closed-form exchangeable law has complexity
$\Theta_k(n^{1/k})$ and likelihood ratio below $e^3$.  Its probabilities are
transcendental, so finding a comparably transparent rational or finite-precision
family remains open.  Such bounds are intrinsically distributional, since the
support recognizer for every distribution in this section is the constant-one
circuit.

\subsection{Interior versus closure lifts}
\label{sec:interior-vs-closure}

The definition of a $k$-localization allows the lifted joint law to sit on a
proper face of the marginal polytope even when the visible marginal is
strictly positive.  The following restriction removes that allowance.

\begin{definition}[Interior localization complexity]\label{def:interior}
Let $D$ have full support on $\bits^n$ and let $k\ge2$.  Define
$\LC_k^{\circ}(D)$ as the least $\ell$ such that some strictly positive
distribution $Q$ on $\bits^{n+\ell}$ whose log-density has multilinear degree
at most $k$ satisfies $\operatorname{marg}_XQ=D$, with
$\LC_k^{\circ}(D)=\infty$ if no such $\ell$ exists.
\end{definition}

A strictly positive law with degree-at-most-$k$ log-density is $k$-local with
the entire marginal polytope as its face, so
\[
  \LC_k(D)\le\LC_k^{\circ}(D).
\]
Every upper construction in this paper---the block-feature lift of
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-lift}, the digit banks of
Theorem~\ref{thm:exchangeable-window}, and the weight-encoding graphs behind
Proposition~\ref{prop:parity} and Corollary~\ref{cor:noisy-parity}---exposes a
proper face: the lifted joint law is a boundary point of the model even when
the visible marginal is strictly positive.  Conversely, every lower bound in
this paper is proved against the closure.  The results here therefore leave
open whether the boundary is ever necessary.  The question is the
localization analogue of the distinction between rank and border rank in
algebraic complexity \cite{bcs97}.

\begin{conjecture}[Boundary lifts help]\label{conj:interior}
For every $k\ge2$ there is a full-support distribution $D$ with
\[
  \LC_k(D)<\LC_k^{\circ}(D).
\]
\end{conjecture}

Two neighboring questions are open as well: whether $\LC_k^{\circ}(D)$ is
finite for every full-support law, and whether the gap can grow with $n$.
Noisy parity is a concrete candidate for a growing gap:
Corollary~\ref{cor:noisy-parity} localizes $D_{n,t}$ with
$\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$ bits by exposing the weight-encoding graph, and we
know of no interior quadratic localization of comparable size.  A debordering
theorem---an upper bound on $\LC_k^{\circ}$ in terms of $\LC_k$ and
$n$---would transfer every closure lower bound in this paper to positive
architectures, including the strictly positive general Boltzmann machine.

\section{A zero-temperature lower-bound transfer}
\label{sec:zero-temperature}

The polynomial method sees only supports.  A different limit imports
auxiliary-variable lower bounds for pseudo-Boolean optimization into the
full-support regime.  For any function $f:\bits^n\to\mathbb R$, define its
degree-$k$ auxiliary complexity by
\[
  \operatorname{aux}_k(f)
  =\min\left\{\ell:
  f(x)=\min_{h\in\bits^\ell}g(x,h)
  \text{ for some }\deg(g)\le k\right\}.
\]
Thus $\operatorname{aux}_2$ is the usual unrestricted quadratization
complexity.  For $0<t<1$, let
\[
  D_{f,t}(x)=\frac{t^{f(x)}}{\sum_{z\in\bits^n}t^{f(z)}}.
\]
Every $D_{f,t}$ has full support.

The basic sum-to-min passage is classical in tropical algebraic statistics:
large-parameter marginal maps tropicalize to inference functions, and this has
been used to study graphical and hidden-variable model dimensions
\cite{pachtersturmfels04,cuetomortonsturmfels10,montufarmorton17}.  The result
below is not a new assertion that marginalization tropicalizes.  Its content is
the hidden-bit lower-bound comparison across arbitrary boundary faces.  The
elementary closedness fact below replaces any need for a definable choice of
localizations or a Puiseux expansion.

\begin{lemma}[Closed finite tropical image]
\label{lem:closed-tropical-image}
Let $T\subseteq X\times H$ be finite with every fiber
$T_x=\{h:(x,h)\in T\}$ nonempty, and let $V\le\mathbb R^T$ be a linear
subspace.  Then
\[
  \Phi_T(V)=
  \left\{
    \left(\max_{h\in T_x}v(x,h)\right)_{x\in X}:v\in V
  \right\}
\]
is a closed finite union of polyhedral cones.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For every selector $\sigma$ with $\sigma(x)\in T_x$, let
\[
  C_\sigma=
  \{v\in V:v(x,\sigma(x))\ge v(x,h)\text{ for all }x,h\in T_x\}.
\]
This is a polyhedral cone, and on it $\Phi_T$ is the linear map
$v\mapsto(v(x,\sigma(x)))_x$.  A linear image of a polyhedral cone is a
closed polyhedral cone.  There are finitely many selectors, and their cones
cover $V$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Zero-temperature transfer]
\label{thm:zero-temperature}
For every real-valued $f$ and every $k\ge2$,
\[
  \liminf_{t\downarrow0}\LC_k(D_{f,t})
  \ge \operatorname{aux}_k(f).
\]
Equivalently, for all sufficiently small positive $t$,
\[
  \LC_k(D_{f,t})\ge\operatorname{aux}_k(f).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Adding a constant to $f$ changes neither side, so assume $\min_x f(x)=0$.
Since $\operatorname{aux}_k(f)$ is a finite integer, failure of the eventual
inequality would make one fixed
$\ell<\operatorname{aux}_k(f)$ occur at arbitrarily small temperatures.

\emph{Step 1: stabilize one support face along a subsequence.}
Suppose that some $\ell<\operatorname{aux}_k(f)$ localizes $D_{f,t}$ for
arbitrarily small $t$.  Choose a sequence $t_j\downarrow0$ and corresponding
$\ell$-bit localizations $Q_j$; fewer bits may be padded by deterministic-zero
bits.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:face-gibbs}, each support is the inverse image of a
face of the fixed finite polytope $P_k(n+\ell)$.  Passing to a subsequence
makes the support a fixed set $T$.  Every visible fiber $T_x$ is nonempty
because $D_{f,t_j}$ has full support.

\emph{Step 2: pass to the tropical limit on the fixed face.}
Let $V_T$ be the space of restrictions to $T$ of degree-at-most-$k$
polynomials.  The face--Gibbs form gives $\log Q_j|_T\in V_T$.  Put
$s_j=-\log t_j$ and
\[
  v_j=\frac{\log Q_j|_T}{s_j}\in V_T.
\]
For every $x$, the finite log-sum-exp bound gives
\[
  0\le
  \frac{\log D_{f,t_j}(x)}{s_j}
  -\max_{h\in T_x}v_j(x,h)
  \le\frac{\log|T_x|}{s_j}.
\]
The leftmost difference is written in this order because
$D_{f,t_j}(x)=\sum_{h\in T_x}Q_j(x,h)$.  Since
\[
  \frac{\log D_{f,t_j}(x)}{s_j}
  =-f(x)-\frac{
    \log\sum_z t_j^{f(z)}}{s_j}
  \longrightarrow-f(x),
\]
we have $\Phi_T(v_j)\to-f$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:closed-tropical-image} supplies
$v\in V_T$ with
\[
  -f(x)=\max_{h\in T_x}v(x,h).
\]
Choose a degree-at-most-$k$ polynomial $g$ whose restriction to $T$ is
$-v$.  Then $f(x)=\min_{h\in T_x}g(x,h)$.

\emph{Step 3: remove the face constraint by an exposing penalty.}
The fixed support $T$ is facial, so Corollary~\ref{cor:ground-state} supplies
a nonnegative degree-at-most-$k$ polynomial $E$ whose zero set is exactly
$T$.  Choose
\[
  M>
  \max\left(
    \{0\}\cup
    \left\{\frac{f(x)-g(x,h)}{E(x,h)}:(x,h)\notin T\right\}
  \right).
\]
Then $g+ME$ has unconstrained hidden minimum $f$, contradicting
$\ell<\operatorname{aux}_k(f)$.
\end{proof}

Figure~\ref{fig:zero-temperature} isolates the three operations in the proof:
select a stable face, tropicalize the positive marginal, and remove the face
constraint by a penalty.

This theorem is a one-way transfer: an arbitrary degree reduction preserves a
minimum, not the complete finite-temperature partition sum.  Its advantage is
that every lower bound on the unrestricted quantity $\operatorname{aux}_k(f)$,
with no arithmetic restriction on $f$, now yields a distributional lower bound
without any support complexity.

The next result gives a well-conditioned lower bound for a target family at
visible inverse temperature one, even though localization is measured against
the full closure and may use boundary joint lifts.  It lies inside one of the
most classical structured classes of distributions.  Call $D$ on $\bits^n$
\emph{exchangeable} if $D(x)$ depends only on $|x|$.

\begin{lemma}[Facial conditioning]
\label{lem:facial-conditioning}
Let $C\subseteq\bits^n$ be the zero set of a nonnegative degree-at-most-$k$
polynomial.  If $D(C)>0$, then
\[
  \LC_k(D(\mathord\cdot\mid C))\le \LC_k(D).
\]
The same bound holds after pulling the conditional law back along a bijection
$\psi:\bits^m\to C$ obtained by duplicating coordinates and fixing
coordinates to constants.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $Q(x,h)$ localize $D$, and use Theorem~\ref{thm:face-gibbs} to choose a
nonnegative degree-$k$ energy $E_Q$ exposing its support and a degree-$k$
potential $\theta_Q$ for its positive weights.  If $E_C$ exposes $C$, then
$E_Q(x,h)+E_C(x)$ exposes
$\supp(Q)\cap(C\times\bits^\ell)$, and $\theta_Q$ remains a Gibbs potential
there after changing the normalizing constant.  This is a localization of the
conditional law with the same hidden bits.  Substituting duplicated or fixed
Boolean coordinates into the energy and potential does not increase their
multilinear degree, proving the pullback assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exchangeable scale and a well-conditioned analytic family]
\label{thm:exchangeable-window}
For every $k\ge2$ and every exchangeable distribution $D$ on $\bits^n$,
\[
  \LC_k(D)
  \le k\left\lceil(n+1)^{1/k}\right\rceil.
  \tag{1}
\]
Put
\[
  r_n=2^{\lfloor\log_2(n+1)\rfloor},
  \qquad m_n=\log_2 r_n,
  \qquad \alpha_n=2^{1/r_n},
\]
and define the fixed full-support law
\[
  D_n(x)=
  \frac{\exp(-\alpha_n^{|x|})}
       {\sum_{z\in\bits^n}\exp(-\alpha_n^{|z|})}.
  \tag{2}
\]
If
\[
  q_{n,k}=\min\{\ell\ge0:d_k(m_n+\ell)\ge r_n\},
\]
then
\[
  \LC_k(D_n)\ge q_{n,k}.
  \tag{3}
\]
Consequently, for every fixed $k$,
\[
  \LC_k(D_n)=\Theta_k(n^{1/k}),
  \qquad
  \max_xD_n(x)/\min_xD_n(x)<e^3.
  \tag{4}
\]
Thus the worst-case exchangeable scale is $\Theta_k(n^{1/k})$, witnessed by
one closed-form family with a universal likelihood-ratio bound.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Part I: the radial lookup upper bound.}
Write $D(x)=p_{|x|}$, allowing zero values,
put $L=\lceil(n+1)^{1/k}\rceil$, and introduce $k$ groups of hidden bits
$y_{j,a}$, where $0\le j<k$ and $0\le a<L$.  The nonnegative quadratic
polynomial
\[
  P(x,y)=
  \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(1-\sum_{a=0}^{L-1}y_{j,a}\right)^2
  +\left(
    |x|-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}L^j\sum_{a=0}^{L-1}a y_{j,a}
  \right)^2
\]
exposes the graph of the unique base-$L$ expansion of $|x|$, since
$n<L^k$.  For $\mathbf a=(a_0,\ldots,a_{k-1})$, write
$w(\mathbf a)=\sum_jL^ja_j$.  Adding the nonnegative degree-$k$ lookup
\[
  R(y)=
  \sum_{\substack{w(\mathbf a)\le n\\p_{w(\mathbf a)}=0}}
  \prod_{j=0}^{k-1}y_{j,a_j}
\]
restricts the graph to $\supp(D)$.  On that graph the degree-$k$ potential
\[
  \theta(y)=
  \sum_{\substack{w(\mathbf a)\le n\\p_{w(\mathbf a)}>0}}
  (\log p_{w(\mathbf a)})
  \prod_{j=0}^{k-1}y_{j,a_j}
\]
equals $\log D(x)$.  The face--Gibbs theorem now gives (1) using $kL$ hidden
bits.

\emph{Part II: the conditioned diagonal lower bound.}
Abbreviate $r=r_n$, $m=m_n$, and $\alpha=\alpha_n$.
Use $r-1$ visible coordinates in blocks $B_0,\ldots,B_{m-1}$ of sizes
$1,2,4,\ldots,2^{m-1}$.  Their total size is
$\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}2^j=r-1\le n$, so set the remaining $n-r+1$ coordinates to
zero.  The set $C$ on which every block is constant is a diagonal copy of
$\bits^m$ that is quadratically exposed in the order-$k$ feature polytope by
\[
  E_C(x)=
  \sum_{i\text{ fixed}}x_i+
  \sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\sum_{i\in B_j\setminus\{b_j\}}
  (x_i-x_{b_j})^2,
\]
where $b_j\in B_j$.  Pulling $D_n(\mathord\cdot\mid C)$ back to
$u\in\bits^m$ gives unnormalized weights
\[
  w_j=\exp(-\alpha^j),
  \qquad 0\le j<r,
  \tag{5}
\]
because the block-constant point indexed by $u$ has Hamming weight
$j=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}2^iu_i$.

The polynomial $X^r-2$ is irreducible by Eisenstein's criterion at $2$.
Hence $1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{r-1}$ are linearly independent over
$\mathbb Q$.  It follows from the Lindemann--Weierstrass theorem that the
numbers in (5) are algebraically independent over $\mathbb Q$
\cite[Theorem~3.2]{popescu24}.  Indeed, after collecting monomials, a polynomial relation
would give
\[
  \sum_{\mathbf a}c_{\mathbf a}
  \exp\!\left(-\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}a_j\alpha^j\right)=0,
  \qquad c_{\mathbf a}\in\mathbb Q.
\]
Distinct multi-indices $\mathbf a$ give distinct algebraic exponents by the
power-basis independence, while Lindemann--Weierstrass makes their
exponentials linearly independent over the algebraic numbers.

Suppose $\LC_k(D_n)\le\ell<q_{n,k}$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:facial-conditioning}
would give an $\ell$-bit localization of the normalized table in (5).  But
$d_k(m+\ell)<r=2^m$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:algebraic-certificate} supplies a nonzero homogeneous
$H\in\mathbb Z[p_u:u\in\bits^m]$ that vanishes on every such marginal,
including lifts on boundary faces.  Algebraic independence gives
$H((w_j)_j)\ne0$.  If $Z=\sum_jw_j$ and $\Delta=\deg H$, then homogeneity gives
$H((w_j/Z)_j)=Z^{-\Delta}H((w_j)_j)\ne0$, so normalization introduces no
loophole.
This contradiction proves (3).

\emph{Part III: asymptotics.}
Finally, $d_k(s)=\Theta_k(s^k)$, $r>(n+1)/2$, and $m=O(\log n)$, so
$q_{n,k}=\Theta_k(n^{1/k})$; combine this with (1).  Also
$1\le\alpha^{|x|}<4$ because $n<2r$, proving the likelihood-ratio bound.
\end{proof}

The $k=2$ upper bound is the distributional form of the two-digit symmetric
quadratization of Boros, Crama, and Rodr\'iguez-Heck
\cite[Theorem~6]{bcrh20}; the $k$-digit lookup is its direct higher-locality
extension.  The block identification follows Anthony, Boros, Crama, and
Gruber \cite[Lemma~5.1]{anthony16}.  The new ingredients are facial
conditioning, the Eisenstein--Lindemann specialization, and the resulting
inverse-temperature-one target-law localization lower bound.

\section{Dense-table recognition}
\label{sec:recognition}

The face--Gibbs description also gives a finite decision procedure without
computing a marginal ideal.  Algebraic invariants and semialgebraic membership
tests are classical for fixed latent graphical models: Critch gives such a
test for binary hidden Markov models \cite{critch13}, while Seigal and
Mont\'ufar give an explicit semialgebraic description of a small RBM
\cite{seigalmontufar18}.  Our problem instead asks uniformly over the full
order-$k$ interaction model, a prescribed binary latent budget, and all lifted
support faces.  The contribution below is the resulting exact algorithm and
bit-complexity bound, not the general notion of model membership.  The input
model matters here: the following
statements concern a dense exact table: all $2^n$ probabilities, including
zeros, are listed as binary numerator--positive-denominator pairs.  They do
not concern samples or a succinct probability circuit.  Write
$\lVert D\rVert_{\rm bit}$ for the table's total bit length.

\begin{theorem}[Exact rational-table recognition]
\label{thm:etr-recognition}
Let $D$ be a rational distribution on $\bits^n$, let $k\ge2$ and $\ell\ge0$,
and put $N=n+\ell$.  In time polynomial in
\[
  \lVert D\rVert_{\rm bit}+2^N d_k(N)
\]
one can construct an existential formula over the reals that is satisfiable if
and only if $\LC_k(D)\le\ell$.  Consequently, for every fixed $k$ and constant
$c$, the language of well-formed pairs $(D,\ell)$ satisfying
$\ell\le cn$ and $\LC_k(D)\le\ell$ belongs to $\exists\mathbb R$; malformed
tables and pairs with $\ell>cn$ are rejected in polynomial time.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Introduce one variable triple $(q_y,e_y,w_y)$ per lifted state $y$: the
$e_y$ range over the values of a degree-$k$ energy, the $w_y$ over
trace-Gibbs weights coded by positive parameters, and complementarity
constraints force $q_y$ to be positive exactly where $e_y=0$ and to equal
$w_y$ there.  Vertexwise nonnegativity of $e$ makes its zero set an exposed
face, so Theorem~\ref{thm:face-gibbs} gives soundness; the face--Gibbs form
of any localization gives completeness, with unused hidden budget handled by
padding.  The encoding length is polynomial in the stated quantity.
Appendix~\ref{app:etr-encoding} gives the full formula and the size
accounting.
\end{proof}

The direct formula proves membership in $\exists\mathbb R$, but it introduces
variables for all $2^N$ lifted states.  Stratifying first by support face leaves
only $d_k(N)-1$ continuous Gibbs parameters and gives a stronger bit-model
algorithm.

\begin{lemma}[Enumeration and recovery of facial supports]
\label{lem:face-enumeration}
Put
\[
  V=\{\chi_k(y):y\in\bits^N\},\qquad
  M=2^N,\qquad d=d_k(N),\qquad P=\operatorname{conv}V.
\]
Then $\dim\operatorname{aff}P=d-1$.  Every nonempty face $F$ of $P$ has a
set $B\subseteq\bits^N$ such that
\[
  |B|=\dim F+1\le d,
  \qquad
  \operatorname{aff}\chi_k(B)=\operatorname{aff}F,
\]
and $F$ is the smallest face containing $\chi_k(B)$.

For any nonempty $B\subseteq\bits^N$ with $|B|\le d$, let $F_B$ be that
smallest face and put $T_B=\chi_k^{-1}(F_B)$.  For $y\in\bits^N$, the
condition $y\notin T_B$ is equivalent to feasibility of the rational LP
\[
  \langle a,\chi_k(b)\rangle=0\quad(b\in B),\qquad
  \langle a,\chi_k(z)\rangle\ge0\quad(z\in\bits^N),\qquad
  \langle a,\chi_k(y)\rangle\ge1.
  \tag{6}
\]
Consequently, all nonempty facial supports can be enumerated, with
duplication, using
\[
  C(M,d)=\sum_{j=1}^d\binom Mj
\]
candidate sets and $MC(M,d)$ rational LPs, in total bit complexity
$(Md)^{O(d)}\operatorname{poly}(N)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The evaluations on the Boolean cube of the distinct multilinear monomials of
degree at most $k$ are linearly independent, by Boolean M\"obius inversion.
Thus $V$ spans $\mathbb R^d$ linearly, while the constant feature is identically
one, proving $\dim\operatorname{aff}P=d-1$.  The singleton features make
$\chi_k$ injective, and the affine Hamming-distance functional
\[
  z\longmapsto
  \sum_{i:y_i=0}z_i+\sum_{i:y_i=1}(1-z_i)
\]
uniquely minimizes at $y$, so every element of $V$ is a vertex.

A $q$-dimensional face has $q+1$ affinely independent vertices spanning its
affine hull.  Their preimages form the required $B$: the smallest face
containing them is contained in $F$ and has affine hull containing
$\operatorname{aff}F$, hence equals $F$.

If (6) is feasible, its zero hyperplane exposes a face
containing $B$ and excluding $y$.  Conversely, if $y\notin T_B$, expose
$F_B$, use the constant feature to shift the minimum to zero, and divide by
the positive value at $\chi_k(y)$.  This gives a feasible LP vector.
Each LP has $d$ variables, at most $M+d+1$ constraints, and explicit binary
encoding length $O(Md+N)$, so exact rational LP feasibility is polynomial in
that length.  Finally, $C(M,d)\le dM^d$, which gives the stated total bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Bit complexity of existential real feasibility]
\label{lem:etr-bit-bound}
Consider an existential sentence in $v$ real variables whose quantifier-free
part is a Boolean combination of sign conditions on $s$ integer polynomials.
Suppose their degrees are at most $\delta$, their coefficient bit sizes are at
most $\tau$, and the total sparse binary encoding length is $\Lambda$.  The
sentence can be decided deterministically in
\[
  (\Lambda+\tau)^{O(1)}(s\delta)^{O(v)}
  \tag{$\mathsf{ETR}$}
\]
bit operations.  The bound permits strict inequalities and arbitrary
quantifier-free Boolean combinations.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The existential decision bound of Basu, Pollack, and Roy uses
$s^{v+1}\delta^{O(v)}$ arithmetic operations and integers of bit size at most
$\tau\delta^{O(v)}$ \cite[Theorem~13.13, p.~516]{basu06}; see also
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{renegar92}.  Implementing those integer operations in the
bit model and accounting for the sparse formula encoding gives
$\mathsf{ETR}$.  Rational coefficients are handled by clearing positive
denominators.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Quasipolynomial dense-table recognition]
\label{thm:qp-recognition}
Let $D$ be a dense rational probability table on $\bits^n$, let $k\ge2$ and
$\ell\ge0$,
and put
\[
  N=n+\ell,\qquad M=2^N,\qquad d=d_k(N).
\]
There is a deterministic bit-model algorithm deciding whether
$\LC_k(D)\le\ell$ in time
\[
  \operatorname{poly}(\lVert D\rVert_{\rm bit}+N)(Md)^{O(d)}.
  \tag{\(\star\)}
\]
Consequently, for every fixed $k$ and $c$, the restriction to
$\ell\le cn$ is decidable, for total input length $L$, in time
\[
  L^{O_{k,c}(\log^k L)}
  =\exp\!\bigl(O_{k,c}(\log^{k+1}L)\bigr).
\]
In particular, $k=2$, $\ell=1$ recognition is in
$\operatorname{TIME}(\exp(O(\log^3L)))$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: enumerate candidate facial supports.}
Reject malformed tables first, and assume henceforth that
$S=\supp(D)$ is nonempty.  Lemma~\ref{lem:face-enumeration} enumerates every
nonempty facial support $T\subseteq\bits^N$, possibly with duplication, using
at most $C(M,d)\le dM^d$ candidates and total LP work $(Md)^{O(d)}$.

\emph{Step 2: decide exact Gibbs feasibility on one face.}
Fix one recovered nonempty face $T$ and reject it unless its visible
projection is exactly $S$.  On $T$, every positive trace-Gibbs weight has,
up to a common factor, the form
\[
  z_y(\theta)=
  \prod_{\substack{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq[N]\\|A|\le k}}
  \theta_A^{y_A},
  \qquad \theta_A>0.
\]
For $x\in S$, put
\[
  S_x(\theta)=\sum_{h:(x,h)\in T}z_{(x,h)}(\theta).
\]
Choose $x_0\in S$ and write $D(x)=p_x/r_x$ with $r_x>0$.  The normalized
trace law has visible marginal $D$ exactly when the following system is
feasible:
\[
  \theta_A>0,\qquad
  p_{x_0}r_xS_x(\theta)-p_xr_{x_0}S_{x_0}(\theta)=0
  \quad(x\in S).
  \tag{7}
\]
These equations say that the positive vector $(S_x)_x$ has the same ratios as
$D$; normalization then makes the vectors equal.  Hence the face--Gibbs
theorem proves both soundness and completeness of this test.

\emph{Step 3: account for bit complexity.}
For system (7), the parameters in Lemma~\ref{lem:etr-bit-bound} satisfy
\[
  v=d-1,\qquad s\le M+d,\qquad \delta\le d-1,
\]
and its sparse encoding length is
$O(Md+\lVert D\rVert_{\rm bit})$.  Indeed, across the
$S_x$, every lifted state contributes once; repeated appearances of
$S_{x_0}$ contribute at most $|S|2^\ell\le M$ further monomials.  Its sparse
encoding therefore has $O(Md)$ symbol occurrences and coefficient bit length
polynomial in $\lVert D\rVert_{\rm bit}$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:etr-bit-bound}
therefore decides (7) in
$(Md)^{O(d)}\operatorname{poly}(\lVert D\rVert_{\rm bit})$ bit operations
per recovered face.  Multiplying by the $dM^d$ candidate faces and including
their LP work preserves (\(\star\)).  A localization using
fewer than $\ell$ bits may be padded with hidden bits exposed at zero, so the
test decides the budget inequality.

\emph{Step 4: substitute the dense-table parameters.}
Finally, when $\ell\le cn$, dense input gives $M\le L^{c+1}$,
$N=O_c(\log L)$, and $d=O_{k,c}(\log^kL)$.  Substitution in
(\(\star\)) proves the stated bound.
\end{proof}

For full-support tables and a fixed hidden budget, the visible-only
coefficients can also be eliminated.  This removes one power of $\log L$ from
the exponent.

\begin{theorem}[Few-variable full-support recognition]
\label{thm:full-support-recognition}
Fix $k\ge2$ and $\ell\ge1$, and put
\[
  r=d_k(n+\ell)-d_k(n).
\]
Given a strictly positive dense rational probability table $D$ of total bit
length $L$,
whether $\LC_k(D)\le\ell$ can be decided deterministically in time
\[
  L^{O_{k,\ell}(r+1)}
  =L^{O_{k,\ell}(\log^{k-1}L)}
  =\exp\!\bigl(O_{k,\ell}(\log^kL)\bigr).
\]
For $k=2$ and $\ell=1$, the bound is $\exp(O(\log^2L))$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $n\le k$, then $\log D$ has multilinear degree at most $n\le k$, so $D$
is already a zero-latent $k$-local Gibbs law.  Hence assume $n\ge k+1$.
Separate the visible-only and hidden-dependent monomials in an exposing
energy and a trace Gibbs potential:
\[
  E(x,h)=A(x)+B(x,h),\qquad
  G(x,h)=C(x)+J(x,h),
  \tag{8}
\]
where every monomial in $B$ or $J$ contains a hidden coordinate.  There are
exactly $r$ such monomials.  Full visible support forces every fiber of the
zero set of $E$ to be nonempty, so nonnegativity gives
\[
  A(x)=-\min_hB(x,h),\qquad
  H_x=\operatorname*{argmin}_hB(x,h).
  \tag{9}
\]
Thus $A$ need not be quantified.

Exponentiate the $r$ coefficients of $J$ to positive variables $u_R$.  For
each hidden assignment put
\[
  W_h(x;u)=\prod_Ru_R^{(x,h)_R},
  \qquad
  R_{B,u}(x)=\sum_{h\in H_x}W_h(x;u).
  \tag{10}
\]
The marginal is $D$ exactly when $B,u$ can be chosen so that the two Boolean
functions
\[
  -\min_hB(x,h),\qquad
  \log D(x)-\log R_{B,u}(x)
  \tag{11}
\]
have degree at most $k$.  In that case set
$A(x)=-\min_hB(x,h)$ and
$C(x)=\log D(x)-\log R_{B,u}(x)$.  Then
$E=A+B$ is nonnegative with zero set
$\{(x,h):h\in\operatorname*{argmin}_gB(x,g)\}$, while $G=C+J$ has positive
trace weights whose sum over the fiber above $x$ is exactly $D(x)$.
The face--Gibbs theorem therefore gives the required localization.
Conversely, every full-support localization has (8)--(11), after absorbing its
normalizing constant into the constant term of $C$.

Appendix~\ref{app:full-support-recognition} gives the complete semialgebraic
encoding.  It uses the vanishing of all $(k+1)$-dimensional alternating cube
sums to test degree at most $k$, and a disjunction over the exact minimizer
sets $H_x$ to retain all ties and boundary faces.  The global formula has
$v=2r$ variables and degree $\delta\le2^kr$, while its polynomial count,
sparse encoding length, and coefficient bit size are all
$L^{O_{k,\ell}(1)}$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:etr-bit-bound} therefore decides it in
$L^{O_{k,\ell}(r+1)}$ time.
Since $r=O_{k,\ell}(n^{k-1})$ and $n\le\log_2L$, this is the claimed bound.
Padding handles localizations using fewer than $\ell$ bits.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[ETH barrier to dense-table hardness]
\label{cor:recognition-eth}
Fix $k$ and $c$.  If the language of well-formed dense rational pairs
$(D,\ell)$ with $\ell\le cn$ and $\LC_k(D)\le\ell$ is NP-hard under
polynomial-time many-one reductions, then the Exponential Time Hypothesis is
false.  The same conclusion holds for every fixed-budget restriction,
including $k=2$, $\ell=1$, and therefore also for $\exists\mathbb R$-hardness
of any such restriction.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Compose a hypothetical polynomial-output reduction from sparse $3$-SAT on
$m$ variables with Theorem~\ref{thm:qp-recognition}.  Its output has length
$L=m^{O(1)}$, so recognition takes
$\exp(O_{k,c}(\log^{k+1}L))=2^{o(m)}$, contrary to the sparsification form of
ETH \cite{ipz01}.  Finally, $\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\exists\mathbb R$, so
$\exists\mathbb R$-hardness implies NP-hardness.
\end{proof}

At zero latent bits the logarithms can be eliminated completely.

\begin{proposition}[The zero-latent case is in $\mathrm P$]
\label{prop:zero-latent-p}
Given a dense exact rational probability table $D$ and $2\le k\le n$, deciding
whether $\LC_k(D)=0$ is deterministic polynomial-time, uniformly in $k$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $T=\supp(D)$.  First test feasibility of the rational linear system
\[
  \langle a,\chi_k(x)\rangle=0\quad(x\in T),
  \qquad
  \langle a,\chi_k(x)\rangle\ge1\quad(x\notin T).
  \tag{\(\dagger\)}
\]
It is feasible exactly when $T$ is the inverse image of a face of $P_k(n)$:
one direction is immediate.  Conversely, if $T$ is proper, a nonnegative
exposing functional can be rescaled by its positive minimum off $T$; if
$T=\bits^n$, take $a=0$.

Next let $A_T$ be the matrix with columns $\chi_k(x)$, $x\in T$, including
the constant feature.  Compute a rational basis $z^{(1)},\ldots,z^{(r)}$ of
$\ker A_T$ and clear denominators.  Then
\[
  \log D|_T\in\operatorname{rowspan}_{\mathbb R}(A_T)
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \prod_{x\in T}D(x)^{z^{(j)}_x}=1
  \quad(1\le j\le r).
  \tag{\(\ddagger\)}
\]
For $D(x)=p_x/q_x$ on $T$ and
$z_x=z_x^+-z_x^-$, the corresponding equality in $(\ddagger)$ is exactly
\[
  \prod_{x\in T}p_x^{z_x^+}q_x^{z_x^-}
  =
  \prod_{x\in T}q_x^{z_x^+}p_x^{z_x^-}.
\]
This is equality of products of positive integers with binary-encoded
exponents, decidable in polynomial time by gcd refinement without integer
factorization \cite[Proposition~2.1]{etessami14}; no ordering comparison is
used.  Fraction-free Gaussian elimination produces a rational kernel basis of
polynomial bit length, and clearing denominators preserves polynomial bit
length.  Finally, $d_k(n)\le2^n\le\lVert D\rVert_{\rm bit}$, so the LP, feature
matrix, and all power-product instances have size polynomial in the dense
table input.
Theorem~\ref{thm:face-gibbs} says that faciality $(\dagger)$ together with the
Gibbs condition $(\ddagger)$ is exactly $\LC_k(D)=0$.
\end{proof}

The algorithms above still enumerate the lifted cube, so they do not control
an unrestricted binary-encoded $\ell$.  For a fixed positive budget the
remaining dense-table question is polynomial versus quasipolynomial time, not
conventional NP-hardness unless ETH fails.  None of these results applies to
approximate, sampled, algebraic, or succinctly represented laws.

\section{Circuit and support contrast}\label{sec:circuit-support}
\subsection{Circuit upper bounds}

The following elementary principle is useful independently of the face
theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Local verification]\label{thm:local-verification}
Let $T\subseteq\bits^N$ be nonempty.  Suppose
we select marginals of $U_T$ on scopes of size at most $k$ such that every
$y\notin T$ realizes a zero-probability local pattern in at least one selected
marginal.  Then $U_T$ is $k$-local.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Any feasible distribution is supported on $T$: positive mass at $y\notin T$
would make all of its selected local patterns positive.  Every distribution
supported on $T$ has entropy at most $\log|T|$, with equality at $U_T$.
\end{proof}

Our circuits are finite directed acyclic graphs with free fanout, hardwired
constants, and gates given by arbitrary Boolean truth tables of arity at most
$r$; input wires are free and every computational gate, including the gate
feeding a designated output, is counted.  Output terminals may also copy an
input or gate wire.  Let $G_r(D)$ be the minimum of $m+s$ over exact generators
that map $m$ uniform seed bits to $D$ using $s$ gates.  When no such circuit
exists, set $G_r(D)=\infty$.  Let $C_r(S)$ be the minimum gate count of a
single-output circuit recognizing $S$.

For the universal NAND basis, define $G_{\mathrm{NAND}}(D)$ and
$C_{\mathrm{NAND}}(S)$ by the same accounting, with every computational gate
required to be NAND.

\begin{theorem}[Circuit traces]\label{thm:upper-bounds}
For every $k\ge2$,
\[
  \LC_k(D)\le G_{k-1}(D).
\]
If $D$ is uniform on $S$, then
\[
  \LC_k(D)\le C_{k-1}(S).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a generator, lift each random input to its complete circuit trace and take
the uniform distribution on valid traces.  Treat the random inputs and all gate
values as latent variables, introduce the designated outputs as visible copies,
and impose pairwise equality constraints between output terminals and their
source wires.  For every gate, constrain the marginal on that gate and its at
most $k-1$ parents.  Any invalid trace violates a gate or equality relation, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:local-verification} applies.  The visible marginal is $D$ and
the latent count is $m+s$.

For a recognizer of $S$, take the uniform distribution on accepting traces,
make all gate values latent, use the same gate constraints, and add the unary
constraint that the output is one.  Its visible marginal is uniform on $S$ and
the latent count is the circuit size.
\end{proof}

These constructions use a special case of
Theorem~\ref{thm:face-gibbs}: their exposing energy is a sum of nonnegative gate
penalties, and their within-face Gibbs potential is constant.

The preceding scope-counting argument would assign locality three to an
ordinary binary gate.  Ground-state synthesis removes that loss.  For Boolean
$a,b,c$, put
\[
  \Phi_{\mathrm{NAND}}(a,b,c)
  =3-2a-2b-3c+ab+2ac+2bc.
\]
A direct check shows that this quadratic polynomial is zero exactly when
$c=\operatorname{NAND}(a,b)$ and is strictly positive on the other four
assignments.

\begin{theorem}[Quadratic NAND synthesis]
\label{thm:nand-synthesis}
For every distribution $D$ and every nonempty $S\subseteq\bits^n$,
\[
  \LC_2(D)\le G_{\mathrm{NAND}}(D),
  \qquad
  \LC_2(U_S)\le C_{\mathrm{NAND}}(S).
\]
More generally, any NAND verifier for $S$ with $w$ witness bits and $s$ gates
satisfies
\[
  \GSE_2(S)\le w+s,
\]
where $\GSE$ is the support-only ground-state extension measure defined in
Appendix~\ref{app:support-invariants}.  If the verifier is unambiguous, then
$\LC_2(U_S)\le w+s$ as well.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a NAND circuit, sum one copy of $\Phi_{\mathrm{NAND}}$ for each gate.
For a recognizer, also add the linear penalty $1-o$ for its output wire $o$.
The resulting nonnegative quadratic energy has one zero-energy trace above
each $x\in S$ and none above $x\notin S$.  Its uniform ground-state law is
therefore a quadratic local model whose visible marginal is $U_S$; all gate
values are latent.

For a generator, retain the seed and gate values as latent variables, introduce
the generated coordinates as visible copies of the designated outputs, and
add the quadratic equality penalties $(x_i-o_i)^2$.  There is exactly one
zero-energy trace per seed, so the uniform ground-state law pushes forward to
exactly the output law $D$.  This uses $m+s$ latent bits.

Finally, apply the recognizer construction to the visible input, the witness,
and the verifier trace.  Unambiguity gives exactly one zero-energy lifted state
above each $x\in S$, so its visible marginal is uniform.  With an arbitrary
nondeterministic verifier the same construction still projects onto $S$, but
weights each $x$ in proportion to its number of accepting witnesses.
\end{proof}

This is the binary-circuit instance of classical ground-state synthesis
\cite{guperales12}.  Earlier work gives a different, dynamical comparison:
Parberry and Schnitger simulate clocked Boltzmann machines by threshold
circuits \cite{parberryschnitger89}.  Their resources are time and
threshold-circuit size under an update schedule.  The construction here is
instead a static, exact encoding of an equilibrium ground support and its
witness multiplicities.  More generally, Fourier expansion gives a direct
diagonal Hamiltonian representation of any Boolean or real function
\cite{hadfield21}; that representation need not have bounded locality and does
not optimize a hidden-bit count.  The resource studied here is precisely the
number of auxiliary bits needed while keeping the interaction order fixed.
Its consequence for lower bounds is immediate and important:
\[
  C_{\mathrm{NAND}}(S)\ge \LC_2(U_S).
\]
Every binary Boolean gate has a constant-size NAND implementation, so this
basis-specific complexity is within a constant factor of ordinary fan-in-two
circuit size.  Thus a strong explicit quadratic localization lower bound for a
uniform distribution on a support cannot evade general Boolean-circuit lower
bounds.  Full-support
laws, whose hardness lies in their weights rather than their support, offer a
genuinely different target.

\subsection{A natural-proofs barrier}
\label{sec:natural-proofs}

The counting bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:random-support} shows that almost
every support is hard for the support program.  Razborov and Rudich's
natural-proofs theorem \cite{razborovrudich97} turns exactly this abundance
into a barrier once the certifying property is efficiently computable.
Identify a nonempty support $S\subseteq\bits^n$ with its indicator function.

\begin{proposition}[Natural-proofs status of the support program]
\label{prop:natural-proofs}
Fix $k\ge2$ and a function $s(n)=n^{\omega(1)}$.  Suppose the property
$\Phi=(\Phi_n)$ of nonempty supports $S\subseteq\bits^n$ is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{constructive}: decidable in time $2^{O(n)}$ from the $2^n$-bit
  indicator table of $S$;
  \item \emph{large}: satisfied by at least a $2^{-O(n)}$ fraction of all
  nonempty supports; and
  \item \emph{useful for localization}: every $S$ satisfying $\Phi_n$ has
  $\GSE_k(S)\ge s(n)$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $\Phi$ is a natural property useful against $\mathrm{P/poly}$ in the
sense of Razborov--Rudich.  Consequently, if pseudorandom function families
of exponential hardness exist, then no such $\Phi$ exists---even though
properties satisfying largeness and usefulness alone exist by
Theorem~\ref{thm:random-support}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A degree-$2$ energy has degree at most $k$, so
$\GSE_k(S)\le\GSE_2(S)$ and $\Phi_n$ forces $\GSE_2(S)\ge s(n)$.  If $1_S$
has a fan-in-two Boolean circuit with $t$ gates, replacing every gate by a
constant-size NAND implementation gives a NAND recognizer of $S$ with $O(t)$
gates; viewed as a verifier with zero witness bits,
Theorem~\ref{thm:nand-synthesis} yields $\GSE_2(S)=O(t)$.  Hence every $S$
satisfying $\Phi_n$ has circuit complexity $\Omega(s(n))$, which is
superpolynomial, so $\Phi$ is useful against $\mathrm{P/poly}$.
Constructivity and largeness are hypotheses (1) and (2), transported along
the identification of supports with their indicators; the excluded empty
support corresponds to the single constant-zero function and changes no
fraction by more than $2^{-2^n}$.  The consequence is the Razborov--Rudich
theorem \cite{razborovrudich97}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Where this paper's methods stand]\label{rem:natural-methods}
Largeness is automatic at the generic scale by
Theorem~\ref{thm:random-support}, so the barrier prices only constructivity.
The sign-definite degree $\ndeg_+$ is computable from the table in time
$2^{O(n)}$ by linear programming, hence constructive; it is compatible with
Proposition~\ref{prop:natural-proofs} because
Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-product} lets it certify only logarithmically many
latent bits, far below usefulness.  The exact selector criterion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:selector-closure} eliminates a universal quantifier over
$2^{\ell|S|}$ selectors and is not known to be constructive.  The counting
bound itself computes no property.  Finally, the full-support separations of
Sections~\ref{sec:full-support} and~\ref{sec:zero-temperature} bound no
Boolean function's circuit size, so the natural-proofs framework does not
constrain them; this is the precise sense in which the weight-sensitive
regime evades the barrier.
\end{remark}

\subsection{An unconditional circuit converse}

We now turn the ground-state certificate back into a circuit.  A nondeterministic
circuit for $S\subseteq\bits^n$ is a circuit $C(x,h)$ such that
$x\in S$ if and only if $C(x,h)=1$ for some witness $h$.  Let $\NSize(S)$ be
the minimum witness length plus gate count over fan-in-two circuits in the
convention above.

We need a standard compilation fact.  A linear exact-threshold predicate on
$d$ Boolean inputs tests an equality $\sum_i w_i z_i=t$.  Real weights can be
replaced by integer weights of magnitude at most $d^{d/2+1}$ without changing
the Boolean zero set \cite[Theorem~6]{babai21}.

\begin{lemma}[Exact-threshold compilation]\label{lem:exact-threshold}
A predicate on $N$ bits of the form
\[
  \sum_{A\subseteq[N],\ |A|\le k}a_A y_A=0
\]
has a fan-in-two Boolean circuit of size
\[
  O\bigl(kd+d^2\log d\bigr),
  \qquad d=d_k(N).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First compute the $d$ monomials, using at most $k-1$ conjunction gates per
monomial.  Let $a\in\mathbb R^d$ be the coefficient vector, including the
constant feature, and apply \cite[Theorem~6]{babai21} to the subspace
$V=\ker(a)$.  It gives $w\in\mathbb Z^d$, with
$|w_i|\le d^{d/2+1}$, such that
\[
  w\cdot z=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z\in V
  \qquad(z\in\bits^d).
\]
Restricting this total exact-threshold predicate to the feature image
$z=\chi_k(y)$ preserves precisely the required zero set.  The weights have
$O(d\log d)$ bits; Appendix~\ref{app:threshold-compilation} gives the standard
adder count.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Localization to nondeterministic circuits]
\label{thm:converse}
Let $D$ be a distribution on $\bits^n$ with support $S$, and suppose $D$ has a
$k$-localization with $\ell$ latent bits.  Put $N=n+\ell$ and $d=d_k(N)$.  Then
\[
  \NSize(S)\le \ell+O\bigl(kd+d^2\log d\bigr).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $Q$ be the lifted $k$-local distribution.  By
Corollary~\ref{cor:ground-state}, membership in $\supp(Q)$ is one degree-$k$
exact-threshold test, compiled by Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-threshold}.  On visible
input $x$, guess the $\ell$ latent bits $h$ and accept exactly when
$(x,h)\in\supp(Q)$.  Since $D$ is the visible marginal of $Q$,
\[
  x\in S\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \exists h\in\bits^\ell\; (x,h)\in\supp(Q).
\]
\end{proof}

The same ground-state test can instead be expanded over all witnesses.  This
pays exponentially in the latent count, but the complexity of each fixed
witness slice depends only on the visible feature dimension.

\begin{theorem}[Deterministic witness expansion]
\label{thm:deterministic-converse}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:converse}, put $d_0=d_k(n)$.  Then
\[
  C_2(S)
  \le
  O\!\left(2^\ell\bigl(kd_0+d_0^2\log d_0\bigr)\right).
\]
Consequently, for every fixed $k\ge2$,
\[
  \LC_k(D)
  \ge
  \log_2\!\bigl(1+C_2(\supp D)\bigr)-O_k(\log(2+n)).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $E(x,h)$ be the nonnegative degree-at-most-$k$ exposing energy for the
lifted support.  For each fixed $h\in\bits^\ell$, the predicate
$E(x,h)=0$ is one exact-threshold test of the $d_0$ visible monomials.  Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-threshold} to each slice and take the disjunction over all
$2^\ell$ witnesses.  This recognizes $S$, since
\[
  x\in S
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \bigvee_{h\in\bits^\ell}[E(x,h)=0].
\]
The first bound follows.  For fixed $k$, its multiplicative factor outside
$2^\ell$ is $n^{O(k)}\log(2+n)$; taking logarithms, with harmless additive
ones for constant circuits, gives the second statement.
\end{proof}

Theorems~\ref{thm:converse} and~\ref{thm:deterministic-converse} expose two
different losses.  Nondeterminism retains a polynomial dependence on
$n+\ell$ by guessing the witness.  Deterministic simulation removes the guess
at cost $2^\ell$, but each resulting exact-threshold slice involves only the
visible monomials.  With exact-threshold gates as primitives, these slices
have the normal form
\[
  \operatorname{OR}_{h\in\bits^\ell}
  \circ\operatorname{EQTHR}\circ\operatorname{AND}_{\le k},
\]
where all slice coefficients arise from one jointly degree-$k$ polynomial in
$(x,h)$ rather than from unrelated exact-threshold gates.  This shared
coefficient statement refers to the original real exposing polynomial, before
the slices are separately replaced by bounded integer weights.

\begin{corollary}[Support-complexity lower bound]\label{cor:lower-bound}
For each fixed $k\ge2$, there is a constant $c_k>0$ such that
\[
  \LC_k(D)+n
  \ge c_k\!\left(
    \left(\frac{\NSize(\supp D)}{\log(2+\NSize(\supp D))}\right)^{1/(2k)}
  \right).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use $d_k(N)=O_k(N^k)$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:converse} and invert the resulting
bound
$\NSize(\supp D)=O_k(N^{2k}\log(2+N))$ for $N=n+\LC_k(D)$.
\end{proof}

The exponent reflects the cost of compiling an arbitrary exposing hyperplane
into ordinary Boolean gates.  If exact-threshold gates are allowed as
primitives, the support test uses $O(d_k(N))$ local monomials and the
corresponding exponent improves to $1/k$ up to the chosen gate-cost convention.

\section{Relation to existing models}\label{sec:related}

We use the term localization complexity, but much of the statistical object is
classical.

\paragraph{Hierarchical exponential families.}
For positive distributions, $k$-locality is exactly the order-$k$ hierarchical
log-linear model.  Allowing zeros passes to its topological closure.  Faces of
the marginal polytope characterize the possible boundary supports
\cite{rauh11,malago11}; Theorem~\ref{thm:face-gibbs} packages this fact in the
maximum-entropy language used by localization complexity.  Neighborliness of
these polytopes gives Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse-no-lift}
\cite{kahle10}: sufficiently sparse distributions already lie in the
unlifted closure, for every assignment of weights to their support.

\paragraph{Hidden variables and Boltzmann machines.}
For $k=2$, the joint laws in our definition are exactly the topological closure
of the fully connected, or general, binary Boltzmann-machine model on the
visible and hidden units.  Thus $\LC_2$ is a hidden-unit complexity measure for
that unrestricted closure; the statistical architecture itself is classical.
Moser and Kameda recall universal visible representability with unrestricted
hidden count and prove exponential hidden-unit lower bounds for memorizing
certain visible-vector sets as associative memories \cite{moserkameda92}.
Their lower bound concerns this vector-set memorization criterion, not exact
representation of a prescribed weighted visible law at finite temperature; it
therefore does not imply our generic full-support or exchangeable lower bounds.
A recent regularized construction gives a low-temperature
universal-approximation result while controlling energy minima
\cite{grzybowski23}.

Visible marginals of hierarchical models with hidden variables and their
dimension have also been studied systematically in algebraic statistics
\cite{montufarrauh17,montufarmorton17}.  Their representation theorems transfer
directly in the upper-bound direction.  For example,
Mont\'ufar and Rauh's Corollary~14, together with their bound on the quantities
$D(n,j)$ appearing there, implies that the closure of the order-$r$ model on
$n$ visible bits is contained in the visible marginals of pairwise models with
at most
\[
  \sum_{j=3}^r\binom{n-1}{j-1}
\]
binary hidden units \cite[Corollary~14]{montufarrauh17}.  For a fixed hidden
count, choose for approximation error $1/j$ one corresponding joint model.
The joint probability simplex is compact, so this diagonal sequence has a
convergent subsequence.  The face--Gibbs characterization (equivalently, the
standard closure theorem for finite exponential families) makes its limit
pairwise-local, and continuity of marginalization identifies its visible
marginal with the target law.  Thus their approximation statement gives an
exact closure localization in our convention.

An RBM is a strict subarchitecture: it omits visible--visible and
hidden--hidden edges.  Classical universal approximation places every law on
$\bits^n$ in the closure of an RBM with at most $2^{n-1}-1$ hidden units
\cite[Theorem~1]{montufaray11}.  This is an upper bound for the restricted
architecture.  Conversely, RBM dimension, strong-mode, and
linear-threshold-code lower bounds do not lower-bound $\LC_2$ without a
comparison theorem embedding every unrestricted pairwise hidden model into an
RBM with controlled hidden count.  Theorem~\ref{thm:generic-positive} instead proves the needed parameter
obstruction directly and uniformly across all support faces.  Toric
parameterization, implicitization, invariants, and semialgebraic membership
tests for hidden graphical models are standard algebraic statistics
\cite{geiger06,cuetomortonsturmfels10,critch13,seigalmontufar18}.  The specific
contribution of Theorem~\ref{thm:algebraic-certificate} is the budget-indexed
localization consequence, together with the base-coded escape that computes a
hard Gibbs ray and an effective cold interval.  Boundary safety itself comes
from taking the projective Zariski closure and is not a separate novelty claim.

\paragraph{Extended formulations and nonnegative rank.}
Localization complexity is a hidden-variable extension complexity, so the
extended-formulation literature is the closest complexity-theoretic relative
outside statistics.  There, a polytope is expressed as the projection of a
higher-dimensional one with few facets; by Yannakakis's factorization theorem
the minimum extension size is the nonnegative rank of the slack matrix
\cite{yannakakis91}, unconditional exponential lower bounds are known for
explicit polytopes \cite{fmptw15,rothvoss17}, and psd rank plays the same
role for semidefinite extensions \cite{lrs15}.  The distributional
counterpart of nonnegative rank is the latent class model: the nonnegative
rank of the joint table $[D(x_A,x_B)]$ across a bipartition is the fewest
states of one latent variable rendering the two sides conditionally
independent.  These resources are transverse to ours in both parameters.
First, a latent class model with $m$ states binarizes into
$\lceil\log_2m\rceil$ hidden bits whose lifted log-density has degree
$1+\lceil\log_2m\rceil$: mixtures embed at logarithmic interaction order, not
at fixed $k$.  Second, the rectangle and common-information techniques behind
extension lower bounds are mixture-shaped: they require that conditioning on
the latent state make the visible law a product across the bipartition.
Conditioning a $2$-local lift on its hidden bits leaves arbitrary
visible--visible couplings, so an $\ell$-bit quadratic localization is not a
mixture of $2^\ell$ rectangles, and nonnegative-rank lower bounds do not
descend to $\LC_2$.  They do constrain the RBM subarchitecture, where
conditioning on the hidden layer gives a mixture of at most $2^\ell$ product
laws; the visible--visible and hidden--hidden couplings that separate
$\LC_2$ from RBM hidden-unit complexity are exactly what breaks the rectangle
structure.  No analogue of the slack-matrix factorization theorem is known
for the faces of the $k$-marginal polytope; finding one---an exact
factorization characterization of $\GSE_k$ or $\LC_k$---is the natural route
by which rectangle-style arguments could enter, and is posed as a research
direction below.

\paragraph{Pseudo-Boolean quadratization.}
Quadratization asks for a quadratic $g(x,h)$ satisfying
\[
  f(x)=\min_h g(x,h)
\]
while minimizing the number of auxiliary bits
\cite{anthony17}.  At $k=2$, localization complexity is a distributional cousin:
the support must be the projection of the ground states of a quadratic energy,
and a second quadratic potential controls the relative Gibbs weights on that
ground space.  Quadratization preserves an entire objective; localization
preserves a distribution.  An arbitrary quadratization therefore transfers
only to the ground support of its objective, not automatically to Gibbs
weights.  The universal-set proof is stronger: its auxiliary variables are
fixed monomial features whose graph is quadratically exposed.  That additional
structure yields the exact distributional transfer in
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-lift}.  Lower bounds for preserving all objective
values still do not automatically lower-bound localization, which may discard
every nonminimal value.  Structured constructions can transfer when their
minimizing witness is unique: the even-$n$ parity construction of
\cite{bcrh20}, together with the corrected odd-$n$ construction in
Proposition~\ref{prop:parity}, yields an exact localization.  The correction is
necessary because the odd-$n$ formula~(27) in the authors' open-access version
of \cite[Theorem~11]{bcrh20} fails at $x=1^n$.  With
$L=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$, the quantity inside its square is
\[
  2^L-\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}2^j h_j\ge2,
\]
so the displayed square is at least $4$, although their parity convention
requires minimum zero on this odd-weight input.  Their lower bound and
even-$n$ formula~(26) are unaffected.  The odd-$n$ construction used in
Proposition~\ref{prop:parity} is proved directly and is not attributed to
formula~(27).
Degree-reduction reformulations with an arbitrary target degree have also been
studied for polynomial optimization \cite{gonzalez25}.  They motivate the
order-$k$ comparison, but use equality-constrained program reformulations
rather than exact marginal distributions.

Finite-union parameter obstructions likewise appear in quadratization
\cite[Theorem~1]{anthony17} and binary linearization
\cite[Theorem~6 and Corollary~7]{walter24}.  Our dimension lower bound applies
that style of argument simultaneously to every hidden support face and then
through marginalization.  The exponent is therefore shared with adjacent
representation problems; the distributional content is the exact
graph-exposing lift and its face-uniform converse.
Theorem~\ref{thm:zero-temperature} gives the complementary lower-bound
transfer: minimization does not preserve a finite-temperature marginal, but it
is the valuation of that marginal as temperature tends to zero.  Tropical
marginal maps and inference functions are established
\cite{pachtersturmfels04,cuetomortonsturmfels10,montufarmorton17}; the new step
is the closed finite tropical image across arbitrary boundary faces and the
resulting hidden-bit inequality with unrestricted auxiliary complexity.

\section{Research directions}\label{sec:directions}

These results do not by themselves settle how far localization complexity
separates from neighboring representation measures.  The following are
concrete next tests.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Larger hierarchy gaps.}  Proposition~\ref{prop:hierarchy}
  separates every adjacent pair, but only by one latent bit.  Determine how
  large $\LC_k(D)-\LC_{k+1}(D)$ can be for explicit distributions.
  \item \textbf{Arithmetic robustness of the target family.}
  Theorem~\ref{thm:exchangeable-window} gives a uniformly conditioned
  $\Theta_k(n^{1/k})$ family at inverse temperature one, but its probabilities
  are transcendental.  Find an equally transparent rational or bounded-precision
  exchangeable family with a superlogarithmic lower bound, which will require
  an obstruction beyond algebraic independence.
  \item \textbf{Structured support bounds.}  The selector LP is exact and the
  random Hamming-layer thinning is exponentially hard, but eliminating all
  selectors for an explicit support remains open.  Ordinary rank provably
  loses the needed labeling.  A successful degree-sensitive dual would, at
  $k=2$, have consequences for nondeterministic circuits.
  \item \textbf{Sharper compilation.}  Determine the right circuit class for
  exposing energies.  Threshold or exact-threshold circuits retain the natural
  $N^k$ feature count; standard Boolean circuits introduce a weight-simulation
  loss.
  \item \textbf{Algorithms.}  Theorems~\ref{thm:qp-recognition} and
  \ref{thm:full-support-recognition} leave a polynomial-versus-quasipolynomial
  gap for every fixed positive budget, especially $k=2$, $\ell=1$; the
  zero-latent case is in $\mathrm P$.  Close this gap, avoid explicit expansion
  of the hidden cube, and study sampled or succinct input models separately,
  where the dense-table ETH barrier no longer applies.
  \item \textbf{Robust resources.}  Define coefficient-bounded and
  $\varepsilon$-approximate localization complexity, and determine which
  graph lifts, lower-bound certificates, and circuit comparisons survive.
  \item \textbf{Interior versus closure.}  Resolve
  Conjecture~\ref{conj:interior}: exhibit a full-support law whose closure
  localization is strictly cheaper than every interior localization, or prove
  a debordering theorem bounding the gap
  $\LC_k^{\circ}(D)-\LC_k(D)$.
  \item \textbf{A factorization theorem for faces.}  Find an analogue of
  Yannakakis's slack-matrix characterization \cite{yannakakis91} for
  $\GSE_k$ or $\LC_k$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:natural-proofs} constrains what
  an efficiently computable characterization could certify, so a useful one
  must either be nonconstructive in the Razborov--Rudich sense or apply only
  to structured supports.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

Local marginal data can force global support through an exposed face even when
no local pattern is individually forbidden.  Viewing the model as a
hidden-variable extension of the closure of a hierarchical exponential family
makes the resource precise.  The balanced feature graph and the boundary-safe
marginal variety determine the worst-case scale
$\Theta_k(2^{n/k})$.  Facial conditioning and algebraic independence then give
a closed-form, full-support exchangeable target at inverse temperature one
with the tight structured scale $\Theta_k(n^{1/k})$ and likelihood ratio below
$e^3$.  Marginal ideals turn the dimension obstruction into exact
certificates, while the zero-temperature theorem transfers unrestricted
degree-reduction lower bounds to full-support Gibbs rays.

Relative Gibbs reductions organize these constructions compositionally: a
source probability table serves as a free base measure, fresh local Gibbs
coordinates carry a cost, and $n+\LC_k(D)$ is exactly the cost from the
zero-variable law to $D$.  In this language, facial conditioning is a
zero-cost reduction and circuit traces are reductions from uniform seed laws.

The recognition and circuit results locate this extension measure rather than
drive its principal separation.  Facial stratification gives
quasipolynomial exact recognition for dense rational tables and an ETH barrier
to ordinary hardness in that input model.  Quadratic NAND synthesis and the
reverse support compilation show that a strong uniform-support lower bound
would already cross a Boolean-circuit barrier; the full-support weighted
examples avoid that implication.  The main remaining targets are a rational
or finite-precision analogue of the exchangeable family, a separation between
interior and closure localization, and an explicit support family strong
enough to cross the circuit barrier.

\subsection{Facial covers and coherent witness slices}
\label{sec:support-invariants}

The support face retains more structure than an arbitrary nondeterministic
witness relation.  Call a nonempty $F\subseteq\bits^n$ \emph{$k$-facial} if
there is a canonical multilinear polynomial $p$ of degree at most $k$ such
that $p\ge0$ on the cube and $F=\{x:p(x)=0\}$.  Equivalently,
$F=\chi_k^{-1}(G)$ for a nonempty face $G$ of $P_k(n)$.  For nonempty
$S\subseteq\bits^n$, define its facial cover number by
\[
  \fc_k(S)=\min\left\{r:S=F_1\cup\cdots\cup F_r,
  \quad F_i\text{ is $k$-facial}\right\}.
\]
Also define the degree-$k$ ground-state extension complexity
\[
  \GSE_k(S)=\min\left\{\ell:
  S=\{x:\exists h\in\bits^\ell,\ E(x,h)=0\},\quad
  E\ge0,\quad\deg(E)\le k\right\},
\]
where $E$ is again its canonical multilinear representative.

\begin{proposition}[Facial-cover lower bound]
\label{prop:facial-cover}
If $D$ has support $S$, then
\[
  \LC_k(D)\ge\GSE_k(S)
  \ge\left\lceil\log_2\fc_k(S)\right\rceil.
\]
Moreover $\fc_k(S)\le|S|$, so the facial-cover relaxation by itself is capped
at $n$ latent bits.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
An $\ell$-bit localization has a nonnegative exposing energy $E(x,h)$ by
Corollary~\ref{cor:ground-state}, so it is a ground-state extension of its
visible support.  For each fixed $h$, the nonempty zero set of $E_h(x)$ is
$k$-facial, and these at most $2^\ell$ zero sets cover $S$.  This proves both
inequalities.  Finally, every singleton $\{a\}$ is the zero set of the
nonnegative affine Hamming-distance polynomial
\[
  \sum_{i:a_i=0}x_i+\sum_{i:a_i=1}(1-x_i),
\]
so the singleton cover gives $\fc_k(S)\le|S|$.
\end{proof}

The facial cover forgets that the slices $E_h$ come from one jointly
low-degree polynomial.  The following exact criterion retains that coherence.
For a function on $\bits^\ell$, write
$\operatorname{RM}_{\mathbb R}(\ell,r)$ for the evaluation space of real
multilinear polynomials of degree at most $r$.

\begin{proposition}[Filtered slice criterion]
\label{prop:filtered-slices}
For a nonempty $S\subseteq\bits^n$, one has $\GSE_k(S)\le\ell$ if and only if
there is a family indexed by every $h\in\bits^\ell$ of nonnegative canonical
multilinear polynomials
\[
  p_h(x)=\sum_{\substack{A\subseteq[n]\\|A|\le k}}c_A(h)x_A
\]
such that
\[
  S=\bigcup_{h\in\bits^\ell}\{x:p_h(x)=0\}
\]
and, for every $A$,
\[
  (c_A(h))_{h\in\bits^\ell}
  \in\operatorname{RM}_{\mathbb R}(\ell,k-|A|).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If an extension uses fewer than $\ell$ bits, first let its energy ignore the
additional hidden coordinates.  Given a canonical degree-$k$ energy $E(x,h)$,
collect the coefficient of each visible monomial $x_A$.  Its dependence on $h$
has degree at most $k-|A|$, which gives the stated family.  Conversely,
represent each coefficient function $c_A$ by its unique multilinear polynomial
on the hidden cube and put $E(x,h)=\sum_A c_A(h)x_A$.  Every term has total
degree at most $k$, the slices are nonnegative, and their zero sets have
projection exactly $S$.
\end{proof}

Thus the visible degree-$k$ part is common to every slice, the degree-$(k-1)$
coefficient vectors vary affinely with $h$, and the lower layers follow a full
real Reed--Muller filtration.  Rank bounds on these coefficient tables are
necessary relaxations, but the labeled Reed--Muller membership in
Proposition~\ref{prop:filtered-slices} is exact.  The following dual form turns
the same obstruction into moment-matching certificates.

For nonempty $T\subseteq\bits^N$, define its degree-$k$ facial closure by
\[
  \fcl_k(T)
  =\chi_k^{-1}\!\left(
    \operatorname{face}_{P_k(N)}
      \bigl(\operatorname{conv}\chi_k(T)\bigr)
  \right),
\]
where $\operatorname{face}_{P}(A)$ is the smallest face of $P$ containing
$A$.  For a selector $\sigma:S\to\bits^\ell$, write
$\Gamma_\sigma=\{(x,\sigma(x)):x\in S\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Selector facial-closure duality]
\label{thm:selector-closure}
For every nonempty $S\subseteq\bits^n$,
\[
  \GSE_k(S)\le\ell
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \exists\sigma:S\to\bits^\ell\quad
  \operatorname{proj}_X\fcl_k(\Gamma_\sigma)=S.
\]
Let $U_{\Gamma_\sigma}$ be uniform on the selected graph and put
$m_\sigma=\E_{U_{\Gamma_\sigma}}[\chi_k]$.  For every lifted state $y$,
\[
  y\in\fcl_k(\Gamma_\sigma)
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \text{some distribution $R$ has }
  \E_R[\chi_k]=m_\sigma\text{ and }R(y)>0.
\]
Consequently, $\GSE_k(S)>\ell$ if and only if, for every selector $\sigma$,
there is a distribution with the same moments of order at most $k$ as
$U_{\Gamma_\sigma}$ that puts positive mass above
$\bits^n\setminus S$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that a nonnegative degree-$k$ energy $E$ has zero set $T$ with
$\operatorname{proj}_X T=S$.  Choose one zero-energy witness $\sigma(x)$ for
each $x\in S$.  The face exposed by $E$ contains $\Gamma_\sigma$, so its
smallest containing face does as well and is contained in $T$.  Its projection
therefore contains $S$ and is contained in $S$, proving equality.  Conversely,
the smallest face containing $\Gamma_\sigma$ is exposed by a nonnegative
degree-$k$ affine functional of $\chi_k$.  Its preimage is a ground-state set
whose projection is $S$.

It remains to prove the moment statement.  The point $m_\sigma$ is a strictly
positive convex combination of all vertices in $\chi_k(\Gamma_\sigma)$.
Hence the smallest face containing $m_\sigma$ is exactly the smallest face
containing that selected graph, and $m_\sigma$ lies in its relative interior.
If $\chi_k(y)$ belongs to this face, relative interiority gives, for some
$\varepsilon>0$ and $w\in P_k(N)$,
\[
  m_\sigma=\varepsilon\chi_k(y)+(1-\varepsilon)w.
\]
Expressing $w$ as a convex combination of feature vertices produces the
claimed $R$.  Conversely, if such an $R$ exists, every face containing
$m_\sigma$ must contain each vertex given positive mass by $R$, in particular
$\chi_k(y)$.  The final equivalence follows by projecting this statement.
\end{proof}

The duality can be made into an exact rational linear program.  This is useful
both computationally and for identifying where the remaining difficulty lies.

\begin{proposition}[Selector LP certificates]
\label{prop:selector-lp}
Let $S\subsetneq\bits^n$ be nonempty, let $H=\bits^\ell$, and fix a selector
$\sigma:S\to H$.  Put
\[
  A_\sigma=\{(x,\sigma(x)):x\in S\},
  \quad B=(\bits^n\setminus S)\times H,
  \quad C_\sigma=(S\times H)\setminus A_\sigma.
\]
This selector witnesses $\GSE_k(S)\le\ell$ if and only if the following
rational linear system has a solution $a\in\mathbb R^{d_k(n+\ell)}$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \langle a,\chi_k(y)\rangle&=0 &&(y\in A_\sigma),\\
  \langle a,\chi_k(y)\rangle&\ge1 &&(y\in B),\\
  \langle a,\chi_k(y)\rangle&\ge0 &&(y\in C_\sigma).
\end{aligned}
\tag{$\mathrm{LP}_\sigma$}
\]
If it is infeasible, Farkas' lemma gives rational numbers
$\lambda_y\in\mathbb R$ for $y\in A_\sigma$ and
$\mu_y\ge0$ for $y\notin A_\sigma$ such that
\[
  \sum_{y\in A_\sigma}\lambda_y\chi_k(y)
  +\sum_{y\notin A_\sigma}\mu_y\chi_k(y)=0,
  \qquad
  \sum_{y\in B}\mu_y=1.
\tag{$\mathrm{D}_\sigma$}
\]
Thus $\GSE_k(S)>\ell$ has a finite exact certificate consisting of one such
dual obstruction for every selector.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A ground-state energy is strictly positive on the finite set $B$; after
choosing one zero witness above each point of $S$, divide its coefficients by
the minimum on $B$ to obtain $(\mathrm{LP}_\sigma)$.  Conversely, a feasible
system defines a nonnegative degree-$k$ energy with a zero above each
$x\in S$ and no zero above its complement.  The displayed dual alternative is
Farkas' lemma, with the positive total multiplier on $B$ normalized to one.
All data are integral, so feasible primal and dual certificates may be chosen
rational.
\end{proof}

For a fixed selector, facial closure, moment leakage, and the primal--dual pair
above are therefore exactly computable.  The remaining universal quantifier is
substantial: there are $2^{\ell|S|}$ selectors, and no succinct aggregation of
their dual certificates is presently known.  Ordinary coefficient rank cannot
remove this difficulty.

\begin{lemma}[Unfiltered rank loses the support]
\label{lem:rank-obstruction}
For every nonempty $S\subseteq\bits^n$, there is a cover of $S$ by
nonnegative quadratic polynomials whose coefficient vectors span a space of
dimension at most three.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $L(x)=\sum_{i=1}^n2^{i-1}x_i$, which is injective on the cube.  For each
$a\in S$, the canonical multilinear polynomial
\[
  p_a(x)=(L(x)-L(a))^2
\]
is nonnegative, quadratic, and has zero set $\{a\}$.  Every $p_a$ lies in
$\operatorname{span}\{1,L,\widetilde{L^2}\}$, where the tilde denotes
multilinearization.
\end{proof}

Thus a rank or flattening that forgets how coefficient columns are labeled by
the Boolean witnesses is bounded by three even for an arbitrary support.  The
Reed--Muller degree of that labeling, retained by
Proposition~\ref{prop:filtered-slices}, is the essential information.  At
$k=2$, extracting an explicit growing lower bound from it would already prove
a nondeterministic-circuit lower bound by Theorem~\ref{thm:nand-synthesis}.

A separate face count gives exponentially large complexity on almost every
support, even though it does not exploit the Reed--Muller filtration or the
selector duality.

\begin{theorem}[Counting bound for uniform supports]
\label{thm:random-support}
For every fixed $k\ge2$, there are constants $c_k,c'_k>0$ such that, for all
sufficiently large $n$, a uniformly random nonempty
$S\subseteq\bits^n$ satisfies
\[
  \GSE_k(S)>c_k2^{n/(k+1)}
\]
with probability at least $1-2^{-c'_k2^n}$.  Consequently the same lower bound
holds for $\LC_k(U_S)$ with that probability.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a latent budget $L$ and put $N=n+L$.  The zero set of any nonnegative
degree-$k$ energy on $n+\ell$ bits is the vertex set of a face of
$P_k(n+\ell)$.  This polytope has at most $V=2^N$ vertices and affine dimension
at most $d_k(N)-1$.  If $F$ is a face, then
$F=P_k(n+\ell)\cap\operatorname{aff}(F)$; hence an affine basis of the vertices
of $F$ determines the face.  Such a basis has at most $d_k(N)$ elements, so
the number of faces is at most
\[
  1+\sum_{i=1}^{d_k(N)}\binom Vi
  \le 2^{O_k(N^{k+1})}.
\]
Projection to the visible cube cannot increase the number of supports.  After
summing over $0\le\ell\le L$,
\[
  \#\{S:\GSE_k(S)\le L\}
  \le (L+1)2^{O_k((n+L)^{k+1})}.
\]
Take $L=\lfloor c_k2^{n/(k+1)}\rfloor$ with $c_k$ sufficiently small.  The
base-two logarithm of the last display is then at most, say, $2^{n-1}$ for all
large $n$, whereas there are $2^{2^n}-1$ nonempty supports.  The exceptional
fraction is therefore $2^{-\Omega(2^n)}$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:facial-cover}
transfers the result to $U_S$.
\end{proof}

The same count remains exponential after restricting the support to one
particularly simple local face.

\begin{corollary}[Random thinning of one Hamming layer]
\label{cor:random-middle-layer}
Fix $k\ge2$, put
\[
  A_n=\left\{x\in\bits^n:|x|=\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor\right\},
  \qquad M_n=|A_n|,
\]
and choose $S$ uniformly among the subsets of $A_n$ of cardinality
$\lfloor M_n/2\rfloor$.  There are constants $c_k,c'_k>0$ such that, for all
sufficiently large $n$,
\[
  \GSE_k(S)>c_kM_n^{1/(k+1)}
  =\Omega_k\!\left(
    2^{n/(k+1)}n^{-1/(2(k+1))}
  \right)
\]
with probability at least $1-2^{-c'_kM_n}$.  Consequently the same lower bound
holds for $\LC_k(U_S)$.

In contrast, $\LC_k(U_{A_n})=0$: the entire unthinned layer is the zero set of
the nonnegative quadratic polynomial
\[
  \left(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i-\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor\right)^2.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:random-support} bounds the total number of
supports satisfying $\GSE_k(S)\le L$ by
\[
  (L+1)2^{C_k(n+L)^{k+1}}
\]
for a constant $C_k$.  Take
$L=\lfloor c_kM_n^{1/(k+1)}\rfloor$.  Since
$M_n^{1/(k+1)}\gg n$, choosing $c_k$ sufficiently small makes this count at
most $2^{M_n/3}$ for all large $n$.  On the other hand,
\[
  \binom{M_n}{\lfloor M_n/2\rfloor}
  \ge\frac{2^{M_n}}{M_n+1}.
\]
The exceptional fraction among the half-subsets is therefore at most
$(M_n+1)2^{-2M_n/3}\le2^{-c'_kM_n}$.  Finally,
$M_n=\Theta(2^n/\sqrt n)$, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:facial-cover} transfers the bound to $U_S$.  The displayed
quadratic exposes $A_n$, so its uniform law is already $k$-local.
\end{proof}

For $k=2$, Theorem~\ref{thm:nand-synthesis} gives this counting result a circuit
consequence, but not the quantitative strength of classical Shannon circuit
counting: the present exponent is $n/3$, whereas random Boolean functions need
circuits of order $2^n/n$.  More importantly, a strong explicit lower bound on
$\GSE_2(S)$ would lower-bound nondeterministic NAND verifiers, and a lower bound
on $\LC_2(U_S)$ would lower-bound deterministic NAND recognizers.  The exact
criteria above identify the shared-slice and moment-matching structure that
such an argument would have to exploit.

\subsection{Support lower bounds and parity}

The circuit converse compiles an exposing energy into a Boolean circuit.  A
different elimination of the latent variables gives a simpler algebraic
invariant.  For a Boolean function $f$, let $\ndeg(f)$ be the minimum degree of
a real multilinear polynomial $p$ such that
\[
  p(x)\ne0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(x)=1.
\]
This is the nondeterministic polynomial degree introduced in the quantum-query
literature \cite{dewolf03}.  Define its sign-definite refinement by
\[
  \ndeg_+(f)=\min\{\deg p:p(x)\ge0\text{ and }
  p(x)>0\Longleftrightarrow f(x)=1\}.
\]
Here and below inequalities for a polynomial mean inequalities on the Boolean
cube.  Squaring an ordinary nondeterministic polynomial and multilinearizing
shows
\[
  \ndeg(f)\le\ndeg_+(f)\le\min\{n,2\ndeg(f)\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Nonnegative witness-product bound]
\label{thm:witness-product}
Let $D$ be a distribution on $\bits^n$ with proper support $S$, and let
$\overline{1_S}$ denote the indicator of $\bits^n\setminus S$.  Then
\[
  \ndeg(\overline{1_S})
  \le \ndeg_+(\overline{1_S})
  \le k\,\fc_k(S)
  \le k\,2^{\LC_k(D)}.
\]
Equivalently, every $k$-localization of $D$ with $\ell$ latent bits satisfies
\[
  k2^\ell\ge \ndeg_+(\overline{1_S}).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take an optimal facial cover $S=F_1\cup\cdots\cup F_r$, and let $p_i\ge0$ be
a canonical degree-at-most-$k$ polynomial whose zero set is $F_i$.  Form
\[
  P(x)=\prod_{i=1}^r p_i(x).
\]
After multilinearization on the Boolean cube, $P$ has degree at most
$kr$.  It vanishes exactly on $S$ and is positive on the complement, so it
witnesses $\ndeg_+(\overline{1_S})\le k\fc_k(S)$.  Proposition
~\ref{prop:facial-cover} gives $\fc_k(S)\le2^{\LC_k(D)}$.
\end{proof}

The exponential dependence on the witness length is not merely a defect of
the proof.  The following example realizes it, up to constant factors in the
polynomial degree.

\begin{proposition}[Parity localization]\label{prop:parity}
Let $U_{\mathrm{even},n}$ be uniform on the even-parity strings in $\bits^n$,
where $n\ge1$.  Put
\[
  m=\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor+1,
  \qquad q=\left\lfloor\frac k2\right\rfloor
\]
for $k\ge2$.  Then
\[
  \max\!\left\{0,\left\lceil\log_2\frac nk\right\rceil\right\}
  \le \LC_k(U_{\mathrm{even},n})
  \le
  \left\lceil
    \log_2\left\lceil\frac mq\right\rceil
  \right\rceil.
\]
In particular, for every fixed $k\ge2$,
\[
  \LC_k(U_{\mathrm{even},n})=\log_2 n+O_k(1).
\]
For $k=2$ and $n\ge2$, more precisely,
\[
  \LC_2(U_{\mathrm{even},n})=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil-1.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $p\ge0$ is positive exactly on the odd-parity strings.  The coefficient
of $x_1\cdots x_n$ in its multilinear representation is
\[
  \sum_{x\in\bits^n}(-1)^{n-|x|}p(x).
\]
Only odd-weight strings contribute, and every nonzero summand has the same
sign.  The coefficient is therefore nonzero, proving
$\ndeg_+(\overline{1_{\mathrm{even}}})=n$.  Since odd parity is the complement of the
support of $U_{\mathrm{even},n}$, Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-product} gives
$k2^{\LC_k}\ge n$ and hence the stated integer lower bound.

For the upper bound, let
\[
  b=\left\lceil\frac mq\right\rceil,
  \qquad \ell=\lceil\log_2 b\rceil,
  \qquad W(x)=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i,
  \qquad J(h)=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}2^j h_j.
\]
Consider the nonnegative polynomial
\[
  E(x,h)=\prod_{r=0}^{q-1}
  \bigl(W(x)-2(qJ(h)+r)\bigr)^2.
\]
Its multilinearization has degree at most $2q\le k$.  If $x$ has even weight
$2a$, then $E(x,h)=0$ for the unique witness encoding
$J(h)=\lfloor a/q\rfloor$; odd-weight strings have no zero-energy witness.
Indeed, $0\le a\le m-1$, so
$J(h)\le\lfloor(m-1)/q\rfloor=\lceil m/q\rceil-1=b-1<2^\ell$;
Euclidean division determines the unique factor indexed by
$r=a-qJ(h)\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}$, and binary encoding determines $h$ uniquely
(also when $\ell=0$).
Thus projection of the ground-state set is exactly even parity, with exactly
one ground-state witness over each visible support point.  The uniform
distribution on the ground states is therefore $k$-local by
Theorem~\ref{thm:face-gibbs}, and its visible marginal is
$U_{\mathrm{even},n}$.

For odd $n\ge3$, the preceding construction with $q=1$ already uses
$\lceil\log_2((n+1)/2)\rceil=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil-1$ bits.  We spell out the
even case because uniformity requires more than the minimum-value assertion in
an ordinary quadratization.  Let $n$ be even, put
$L=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$, set
$x'=(1-x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n)$, and use $L-1$ hidden bits in
\[
  E_{\mathrm{ev}}(x,h)
  =\left(
    W(x')-n+2^L-1-\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}2^j h_j
  \right)^2.
\]
This is formula~(26) of Boros, Crama, and Rodr\'iguez-Heck after complementing
one visible bit \cite[Theorem~11 and Remark~3]{bcrh20}.  If $x$ has even
parity, then $W(x')$ is odd and
\[
  W(x')-n+2^L-1\in\{0,2,\ldots,2^L-2\}.
\]
It therefore has a unique expansion using the binary weights
$2,4,\ldots,2^{L-1}$, giving exactly one zero-energy witness.  If $x$ has odd
parity, the displayed integer is odd and cannot equal that even weighted sum.
Thus the zero set projects exactly to even parity with constant multiplicity
one.  This also covers $n=2$, when the hidden sum is empty.  Hence the uniform
ground-state law has the claimed visible marginal.  The lower bound is
$\lceil\log_2(n/2)\rceil=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil-1$ when $n\ge2$.
\end{proof}

The nonnegativity requirement retains the sign-definiteness that
ordinary nondeterministic degree discards; parity shows that this can sharpen
the polynomial degree from about $n/2$ to $n$.  The bound remains deliberately
modest from the viewpoint of circuit complexity: $\ndeg_+(f)\le n$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-product} can force only $\Omega(\log n)$ latent bits.
Strong circuit consequences would require an invariant that also sees the
low-degree dependence of all witness slices $E_h$ on the common witness $h$,
rather than eliminating those slices by their product.

The same method already separates adjacent levels of the locality hierarchy.

\begin{proposition}[Strict adjacent hierarchy]\label{prop:hierarchy}
For every $k\ge3$ and every $n\ge k+1$, let
\[
  S_{k,n}
  =\bigl(\bits^{k+1}\setminus\{1^{k+1}\}\bigr)
    \times\bits^{n-k-1},
  \qquad D_{k,n}=U_{S_{k,n}}.
\]
Then
\[
  \LC_k(D_{k,n})=1,
  \qquad
  \LC_{k+1}(D_{k,n})=0.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The complement indicator of $S_{k,n}$ is $\mathrm{AND}_{k+1}$ on the first
$k+1$ variables, whose nondeterministic degree is $k+1$ \cite{dewolf03}.  If
$D_{k,n}$ had a
$k$-localization with no latent bit, Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-product} would
give $k+1\le k$, a contradiction.  Hence $\LC_k(D_{k,n})\ge1$.

For the matching upper bound, partition the first $k+1$ visible variables into
sets
$A,B$ with $|A|=2$ and $|B|=k-1$, and put
\[
  a(x)=\prod_{i\in A}x_i,
  \qquad b(x)=\prod_{i\in B}x_i.
\]
For one latent bit $h$, define
\[
  E(x,h)=(1-h)a(x)+h b(x).
\]
This polynomial is nonnegative on the Boolean cube and has degree at most $k$.
Its ground states project to $S_{k,n}$: a visible point has a zero-energy witness
exactly when $a(x)=0$ or $b(x)=0$.  Points with $a=b=0$ have two ground-state
witnesses, while points with exactly one of $a,b$ equal to zero have one.

On the ground-state set use the degree-at-most-$(k-1)$ Gibbs potential
\[
  \theta(x,h)=-(\log 2)(1-a(x)-b(x)).
\]
When $a=b=0$, each of the two witnesses has unnormalized weight $1/2$; when
exactly one block product vanishes, the unique witness has weight $1$.
Consequently every $x\in S_{k,n}$ has total unnormalized visible weight one;
the remaining $n-k-1$ bits are free and uniform.
Theorem~\ref{thm:face-gibbs} therefore gives a $k$-localization whose visible
marginal is $D_{k,n}$, proving $\LC_k(D_{k,n})\le1$.

Finally, the degree-$(k+1)$ nonnegative energy
$a(x)b(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{k+1}x_i$ has zero set $S_{k,n}$.  Its uniform ground-state
distribution is $(k+1)$-local without latent variables, so
$\LC_{k+1}(D_{k,n})=0$.
\end{proof}

\section{Further full-support hierarchy separations}
\label{app:full-support-hierarchy}

\begin{proposition}[No-latent characterization]\label{prop:positive-no-latent}
Let $D$ be a full-support distribution on $\bits^n$.  Then
$\LC_k(D)=0$ if and only if there is a multilinear polynomial $p$ of degree at
most $k$ such that
\[
  D(x)=\frac{\exp(p(x))}{\sum_{z\in\bits^n}\exp(p(z))}.
\]
Equivalently, the unique multilinear representation of $\log D$ has degree at
most $k$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
When $D$ has full support, its support face in
Theorem~\ref{thm:face-gibbs} is the entire marginal polytope.  The claimed
Gibbs form is therefore necessary and sufficient.  Normalization changes only
the constant coefficient of $\log D$.
\end{proof}

The next example shows that a single hidden bit can remove one order of a
strictly positive interaction.

\begin{proposition}[Full-support adjacent hierarchy]
\label{prop:positive-hierarchy}
For every $k\ge2$ and every ambient dimension $n\ge k+1$, there is a
full-support distribution $D$ on $\bits^n$ such that
\[
  \LC_k(D)=1,
  \qquad
  \LC_{k+1}(D)=0.
\]
The localization witnessing the first equality may itself be chosen with full
support.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $r=k+1$ and
\[
  z=\frac{(e-1)^r}{4e^{2r}},
  \qquad \gamma=\log z<0.
\]
On $\bits^r$, let
\[
  g(x)=\log\!\left(1+\exp\!\left(\gamma+\sum_{i=1}^r x_i\right)\right).
\]
Write its unique multilinear expansion as
\[
  g(x)=q_0(x)+\lambda\prod_{i=1}^r x_i,
  \qquad \deg(q_0)\le r-1=k.
\]
This displayed choice makes the top coefficient $\lambda$ nonzero.  Indeed,
expanding $\log(1+z e^{\sum_i x_i})$ in powers of $z$ gives
\[
  \lambda
  =\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{(-1)^{m+1}z^m}{m}(e^m-1)^r.
\]
Writing $a=ze^r<1/4$, the absolute value of the tail after the first term is
at most
\[
  \sum_{m\ge2}\frac{a^m}{m}
  \le \frac{a^2}{2(1-a)}
  < z(e-1)^r,
\]
where the last inequality follows from
$z=(e-1)^r/(4e^{2r})$.  Hence $\lambda\ne0$.

Let $h$ be one hidden bit and define the strictly positive joint distribution
\[
  Q(x,h)\ \propto\
  \exp\!\left(-q_0(x)+h\left(\gamma+\sum_{i=1}^r x_i\right)\right).
\]
Its log-density has degree at most $k$: the visible term has degree at most
$k$, and each interaction $hx_i$ is quadratic.  Hence $Q$ is $k$-local.  Its
visible marginal is
\[
  D(x)\ \propto\
  e^{-q_0(x)}\left(1+e^{\gamma+\sum_i x_i}\right)
  =\exp\!\left(\lambda\prod_{i=1}^r x_i\right).
\]
Thus the resulting distribution $D_0$ on $\bits^r$ has full support and
$\LC_k(D_0)\le1$.  Proposition
~\ref{prop:positive-no-latent} and $\lambda\ne0$ rule out a zero-latent
$k$-localization, so $\LC_k(D_0)=1$.  Its log-density has degree $k+1$, making
$D_0$ itself $(k+1)$-local.

For any $n>r$, take $D=D_0\otimes U_{\bits^{n-r}}$.  Tensoring both the
one-bit localization and the zero-latent $(k+1)$-local model with independent
uniform bits preserves their degree bounds.  Conversely, the multilinear
representation of $\log D$ still contains the nonzero degree-$(k+1)$ monomial
$\lambda\prod_{i=1}^r x_i$, so Proposition~\ref{prop:positive-no-latent}
still rules out a zero-bit $k$-localization.  This proves both equalities in
every ambient dimension.
\end{proof}

Marginalizing one binary hidden unit produces a soft-plus term, a standard
device in hierarchical-model representation theory \cite{montufarrauh17}.
The claim here is the resulting exact adjacent-level separation, including the
matching zero-hidden lower bound and the explicit parameter choice, rather
than the soft-plus identity itself.  The hierarchy example has only a one-bit
gap.

\section{Further consequences of the zero-temperature transfer}
\label{app:transfer-consequences}

\begin{corollary}[Noisy parity]
\label{cor:noisy-parity}
Let $\pi_n(x)$ be the odd-parity indicator, let $n\ge2$, and put
$D_{n,t}=D_{\pi_n,t}$.  For each $n$ there is a threshold $t_0(n)\in(0,1)$ such that
every $0<t<t_0(n)$ satisfies
\[
  \lceil\log_2 n\rceil-1
  \le \LC_2(D_{n,t})
  \le \lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil.
\]
Thus any choice of $t_n<t_0(n)$ gives the asymptotic family
$\LC_2(D_{n,t_n})=\Theta(\log n)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Boros, Crama, and Rodr\'iguez-Heck use the convention that parity is one on
even Hamming weight.  Substituting $x_1\mapsto1-x_1$ swaps their convention
with our odd-parity indicator while preserving polynomial degree and the
number of auxiliary variables.  Their lower bound
\cite[Theorem~5]{bcrh20} and the square encodings used in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:parity}---our $q=1$ formula for odd $n$ and their
formula~(26) for even $n$---attain this bound, after complementing one visible
bit as just described.  Thus
$\operatorname{aux}_2(\pi_n)=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil-1$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:zero-temperature} gives the lower bound.

For the upper bound, let $L=\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$ and
$J(h)=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}2^j h_j$.  The quadratic energy
\[
  E(x,h)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i-J(h)\right)^2
\]
exposes the graph of the binary encoding of the Hamming weight, with one
witness above every $x$.  On this graph $\pi_n(x)=h_0$.  Assigning log-weight
$(\log t)h_0$ on the graph and applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:face-gibbs} gives an $L$-bit quadratic localization of
$D_{n,t}$ for every $0<t<1$.
\end{proof}

The transfer also gives a closed-form worst-case family for every locality.

\begin{corollary}[Superincreasing Gibbs rays]
\label{cor:superincreasing}
Fix $n,k,\ell$ with $d=d_k(n+\ell)<2^n$, enumerate the visible cube as
$x^{(0)},\ldots,x^{(2^n-1)}$, and set
\[
  B=\left\lceil d^{d/2}\right\rceil+2,
  \qquad f(x^{(i)})=B^i.
\]
Then $\operatorname{aux}_k(f)>\ell$.  Consequently
\[
  \LC_k(D_{f,t})>\ell
\]
for all sufficiently small $t>0$.  For every fixed $k$, choose a sufficiently
small $c_k>0$ and, for all sufficiently large $n$, set
$\ell_n=\lfloor c_k2^{n/k}\rfloor-n\ge0$ below the dimension threshold.  This
gives an explicit full-support Gibbs ray with an existential hard cold tail
at the asymptotically optimal scale; the cold threshold may depend on
$n,k$, and the ray.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof roadmap]
After choosing one minimizing witness for each visible input, any putative
degree-$k$ reduction would place the superincreasing vector
$(1,B,\ldots,B^{2^n-1})$ in the column space of a $0$--$1$ feature matrix with
only $d$ columns.  An augmented minor is a nonzero integer polynomial in $B$;
Hadamard's inequality bounds its coefficients, and the Cauchy root bound
excludes the displayed choice of $B$.  The complete determinant argument is
given in Appendix~\ref{app:superincreasing-details}.
\end{proof}

The energy values in Corollary~\ref{cor:superincreasing} have enormous dynamic
range.  The family is explicit in the mathematical sense, but not moderately
conditioned or algorithmically convenient, and the proof does not make its
cold threshold effective.  Noisy parity is far more natural, at the price of
only a logarithmic bound.

\section{The direct existential encoding}
\label{app:etr-encoding}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:etr-recognition}]
For each $y=(x,h)\in\bits^N$, introduce variables $q_y,e_y,w_y$.
Introduce coefficients $a_A$ for $|A|\le k$, a positive variable $s$, and
positive variables $u_A$ for $1\le |A|\le k$.  Explicitly impose
$s>0$ and $u_A>0$, and impose
\[
  e_y=\sum_{|A|\le k}a_Ay_A,
  \qquad
  w_y=s\prod_{\substack{1\le |A|\le k\\y_A=1}}u_A.
\]
Here each $y_A\in\{0,1\}$ is input data for the constraint indexed by $y$;
an empty product means $w_y=s$.  For every $y$, also impose
\[
  q_y\ge0,\quad e_y\ge0,\quad q_ye_y=0,\quad q_y+e_y>0,
  \quad q_y(q_y-w_y)=0.
  \tag{\(*\)}
\]
Writing the listed entry $D(x)$ as $p_x/r_x$ with $r_x>0$, finally impose
\[
  r_x\sum_{h\in\bits^\ell}q_{(x,h)}-p_x=0
  \qquad(x\in\bits^n).
  \tag{\(**\)}
\]
These are polynomial equalities and inequalities with integer coefficients.
The displayed products may be replaced by chains of quadratic equations.
Strict inequalities are standard in the existential theory of the reals; in
an equation-only normal form, $p>0$ may instead be written
$\exists r\;pr^2=1$.

The strict constraint in $(*)$ is essential.  Together with complementarity
it forces $q_y>0$ if and only if $e_y=0$.  The marginal equations make this
set nonempty.  Define
\[
  \alpha(v)=\sum_{|A|\le k}a_Av_A,
  \qquad
  F=P_k(N)\cap\{v:\alpha(v)=0\}.
\]
Vertexwise nonnegativity of $e$ makes $F$ an exposed face, and
\[
  \supp(q)=\{y:e_y=0\}=\chi_k^{-1}(F).
\]
Thus $e$ is a nonnegative degree-$k$ energy exposing the support, while on
that support
\[
  q_y=s\prod_{1\le |A|\le k}u_A^{y_A},
  \qquad
  \log q_y=\log s+\sum_{1\le |A|\le k}y_A\log u_A.
\]
Equations $(**)$ normalize $q$ and give it visible marginal $D$.
In particular,
\[
  1=s\sum_{y\in\supp(q)}
  \exp\!\left(\sum_{1\le |A|\le k}y_A\log u_A\right),
\]
so $s$ is the reciprocal partition factor required by the face--Gibbs form.
Theorem~\ref{thm:face-gibbs} therefore proves soundness.  Conversely, its
face--Gibbs form supplies such $a_A,s,u_A$ for any localization.  If that
localization uses $r<\ell$ bits, append bits $h'$ fixed to zero, replace its
exposing energy $E$ by $E+\sum_jh'_j$, and set every Gibbs parameter involving
a padded bit to one.  This preserves the weights and exposes the padded face.

There are $2^N$ lifted states.  Including binary variable and subset indices,
the formula has encoding length
\[
  O\!\left(\lVert D\rVert_{\rm bit}
  +2^Nd_k(N)\operatorname{poly}(N)\right),
\]
which is polynomial in the quantity stated in the theorem because
$N\le\log_2(2^Nd_k(N))$.
If $\ell\le cn$ and $k,c$ are fixed, the explicit table length
$L=\lVert D\rVert_{\rm bit}$ satisfies $L\ge2^n$, and hence
\[
  2^{n+\ell}d_k(n+\ell)
  \le L^{c+1}\operatorname{poly}_k(\log L).
\]
This gives the claimed $\exists\mathbb R$ membership.  No hardness conclusion
is asserted.
\end{proof}

\section{Few-variable full-support recognition details}
\label{app:full-support-recognition}

We spell out the formula used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:full-support-recognition}, including the treatment of tied
minimizers.

\begin{lemma}[Boolean subcube degree test]
\label{lem:subcube-degree}
Let $F:\bits^n\to\mathbb R$.  Its canonical multilinear polynomial has degree
at most $k$ if and only if, for every $I\subseteq[n]$ of size $k+1$ and every
fixing $a\in\bits^{[n]\setminus I}$,
\[
  \sum_{z\in\bits^I}(-1)^{|z|}F(a,z)=0.
  \tag{12}
\]
When $k\ge n$, the condition is vacuous and every function has degree at most
$k$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The left side of (12), up to sign, is the iterated Boolean finite difference
$\Delta_I F(a)$.  If $\deg F\le k$, every such difference vanishes.  Conversely,
write $F=\sum_Sc_Sx_S$.  For fixed $I$, the function
$a\mapsto\Delta_IF(a)$ has canonical coefficients $c_{I\cup J}$ as $J$ ranges
over subsets of $[n]\setminus I$.  Its vanishing at every Boolean $a$ forces
all these coefficients to vanish.  Every $S$ of size greater than $k$ contains
some $I$ of size $k+1$, so $c_S=0$.
\end{proof}

If $n\le k$, every strictly positive table is already a zero-latent
$k$-local Gibbs law, so the recognition algorithm accepts immediately.
For the remainder of this construction assume $n\ge k+1$.

Let $H$ denote the $\ell$ hidden coordinates and put
\[
  \mathcal R=
  \{R\subseteq[n+\ell]:|R|\le k,\ R\cap H\ne\varnothing\},
  \qquad |\mathcal R|=r.
\]
For variables $b=(b_R)_{R\in\mathcal R}$ and
$u=(u_R)_{R\in\mathcal R}$, define
\[
  B_b(x,h)=\sum_{R\in\mathcal R}b_R(x,h)_R,
  \qquad
  W_h(x;u)=\prod_{R\in\mathcal R}u_R^{(x,h)_R}.
  \tag{13}
\]
For every nonempty $K\subseteq\bits^\ell$, choose a canonical $h_K\in K$ and
write
\[
\begin{split}
  \operatorname{ExactMin}_{x,K}(b):={}&
  \bigwedge_{h\in K} B_b(x,h)=B_b(x,h_K)\\
  &{}\wedge
  \bigwedge_{g\in\bits^\ell\setminus K}
  B_b(x,g)>B_b(x,h_K),\\
  R_K(x;u):={}&\sum_{h\in K}W_h(x;u).
\end{split}
\tag{14}
\]
Thus (14) says that $K$ is the complete minimizer set, not merely a selected
subset of minimizers.

Let $Q$ be a $(k+1)$-dimensional Boolean subcube, and partition its vertices
as $Q_+$ and $Q_-$ according to even and odd parity in its free coordinates.
For a tuple $\mathbf K=(K_x)_{x\in Q}$ of nonempty subsets of $\bits^\ell$,
let $\Psi_{Q,\mathbf K}(b,u)$ be the conjunction of
\[
  \bigwedge_{x\in Q}\operatorname{ExactMin}_{x,K_x}(b),
  \tag{15}
\]
\[
  \sum_{x\in Q_+}-B_b(x,h_{K_x})
  =
  \sum_{x\in Q_-}-B_b(x,h_{K_x}),
  \tag{16}
\]
and
\[
  \prod_{x\in Q_+}D(x)
  \prod_{x\in Q_-}R_{K_x}(x;u)
  =
  \prod_{x\in Q_-}D(x)
  \prod_{x\in Q_+}R_{K_x}(x;u).
  \tag{17}
\]
The rational denominators in (17) are cleared.  The promised global
existential formula is
\[
  \exists b\in\mathbb R^r\ \exists u\in\mathbb R^r\quad
  \left[
    \left(\bigwedge_{R\in\mathcal R}u_R>0\right)
    \wedge
    \bigwedge_Q
    \bigvee_{\mathbf K}
    \Psi_{Q,\mathbf K}(b,u)
  \right].
  \tag{18}
\]

For any fixed $b$, the exact minimizer set of $B_b(x,\cdot)$ is unique, so the
disjuncts chosen on overlapping subcubes in (18) are automatically
consistent.  Equations (16) for all $Q$, together with
Lemma~\ref{lem:subcube-degree}, say exactly that
$x\mapsto-\min_hB_b(x,h)$ has degree at most $k$.  Since every term in (17)
is positive, taking logarithms shows that (17) is precisely the alternating
cube identity for
\[
  x\longmapsto \log D(x)-
  \log\sum_{h\in\operatorname*{argmin}_gB_b(x,g)}W_h(x;u).
\]
Thus the second function in (11) also has degree at most $k$.  This proves
soundness of (18); completeness follows by taking the actual minimizer sets
of any localization in (8)--(11).  Strict inequalities in (14) permit ties
inside $K_x$ while excluding precisely the nonminimizers, so boundary joint
supports are included.

There are
$\binom{n}{k+1}2^{n-k-1}=O_k(2^nn^{k+1})$ subcubes.  For fixed $k,\ell$, the
number of tuples $\mathbf K$ per subcube is constant.  Each $R_K$ is a sum of
at most $2^\ell$ monomials of degree at most $r$, so (17) has degree at most
$2^kr$.  Expand the products in (17); because $k$ and $\ell$ are fixed, this
changes the encoding size by only a constant factor per subcube, and clearing
the rational denominators preserves polynomial coefficient bit length.
Formula (18) has $2r$ variables and size polynomial in the dense input length
$L\ge2^n$.  The standard singly exponential real-algebraic bound therefore
gives $L^{O_{k,\ell}(r+1)}$ bit operations.  This completes the complexity
bookkeeping used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:full-support-recognition}.

\section{Exact-threshold compilation details}
\label{app:threshold-compilation}

We complete the gate count in Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-threshold}.  Each of the
$d$ feature monomials uses at most $k-1$ fan-in-two conjunctions, for
$O(kd)$ gates.  The integerized coefficient of each feature has
$B=O(d\log d)$ signed bits.  Fix a two's-complement width
\[
  W=B+\lceil\log_2 d\rceil+2.
\]
This width contains the sum of all $d$ signed terms without overflow.
Hardwire and sign-extend each coefficient to width $W$, then mask it by the
corresponding feature bit; this constructs all conditional summands $w_i z_i$
in $O(dW)=O(dB)$ gates.  Add them in a balanced tree of width-$W$
ripple-carry adders.  Level $j$ has $O(d/2^j)$ additions, so the whole tree
uses $O(dW)=O(dB)$ gates.  The final zero test costs $O(W)$ gates.  Together
with the feature gates this gives
$O(kd+d^2\log d)$ fan-in-two Boolean gates.

\section{Effective marginal-ideal certificates}
\label{app:effective-certificates}

\begin{proof}[Full proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:effective-tables}]
Choose a nonzero homogeneous
$F\in I(\mathcal V_{n,k,\ell})\cap\mathbb Z[p_x:x\in\bits^n]$ of degree
$\Delta$.  Such an $F$ is computable by Theorem~\ref{thm:algebraic-certificate},
and necessarily $\Delta\ge1$.  Enumerate the visible states as
$x_0,\ldots,x_{2^n-1}$ and put
\[
  f(x_i)=(\Delta+1)^i.
\]
Write a degree-$\Delta$ monomial as
$\prod_i p_{x_i}^{\alpha_i}$, where $0\le\alpha_i\le\Delta$ and
$\sum_i\alpha_i=\Delta$.  Under the substitution
$p_{x_i}=t^{f(x_i)}$, its exponent is
$\sum_i\alpha_i(\Delta+1)^i$.  Uniqueness of base-$(\Delta+1)$ expansion shows
that distinct monomials acquire distinct exponents.  Hence
\[
  P(t)=F((t^{f(x)})_x)
\]
is a nonzero polynomial in $t$.

The polynomial $P$ has only finitely many roots, but we can obtain an effective
entire cold interval rather than test isolated values.  Write
\[
  P(t)=t^e\left(a_0+\sum_{j\ge1}a_jt^j\right),
  \qquad a_0\ne0,
\]
and put $A=\sum_{j\ge1}|a_j|$.  If $A=0$, set $\tau=1$; otherwise set
\[
  \tau=\min\left\{1,\frac{|a_0|}{2A}\right\}.
\]
For every real $0<t<\tau$,
\[
  \left|\sum_{j\ge1}a_jt^j\right|
  \le At<\frac{|a_0|}{2},
\]
so $P(t)\ne0$.  The normalized table
\[
  D_t(x)=\frac{t^{f(x)}}{\sum_z t^{f(z)}}
\]
has full support, and it is rational whenever $t$ is rational.  Homogeneity
gives throughout this interval
$F(D_t)=(\sum_z t^{f(z)})^{-\Delta}P(t)\ne0$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:algebraic-certificate} yields $\LC_k(D_t)>\ell$.

For the asymptotic statement, fix $k$ and use
$d_k(N)=\sum_{j=0}^k\binom Nj=O_k(N^k)$.  Choose $c_k>0$ sufficiently small
and, for all sufficiently large $n$, set
\[
  \ell_n=\lfloor c_k2^{n/k}\rfloor-n\ge0.
\]
Then $N=n+\ell_n=\lfloor c_k2^{n/k}\rfloor$ and
$d_k(N)<2^n$.  Applying the preceding effective construction gives a
computable integer energy $f_n$ and a rational $\tau_n>0$ such that every
$0<t<\tau_n$ satisfies
$\LC_k(D_{f_n,t})>\ell_n=\Omega_k(2^{n/k})$.
\end{proof}

\section{The superincreasing determinant argument}
\label{app:superincreasing-details}

\begin{proof}[Full proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:superincreasing}]
Suppose $\operatorname{aux}_k(f)\le\ell$.  A reduction using fewer than
$\ell$ bits can be padded by ignored bits, so write
$f(x)=\min_h g(x,h)$ with exactly $\ell$ auxiliary bits, and choose one
minimizer $\sigma(x)$ for each $x$.  Writing $g$ in the $d$ monomials of degree
at most $k$ gives
\[
  (1,B,\ldots,B^{2^n-1})^\mathsf T=A_\sigma c,
\]
where $A_\sigma$ is the $0$--$1$ feature-evaluation matrix with row
$\chi_k(x,\sigma(x))$ and $d$ columns.  Put
$r=\operatorname{rank}(A_\sigma)\le d<2^n$.
Choose $r$ independent columns $C$ and $r$ rows $R$ on which their minor is
nonzero, and choose a row index $j\notin R$.

Adjoin $v(z)=(1,z,\ldots,z^{2^n-1})^\mathsf T$ to those columns and take the
minor on rows $R\cup\{j\}$.  Its determinant is a nonzero integer polynomial
$P(z)$: the coefficient of $z^j$ is, up to sign, the nonzero minor on
$R\times C$.  Every coefficient is an $r\times r$ determinant of a $0$--$1$
matrix, so Hadamard's inequality bounds its magnitude by
$r^{r/2}\le d^{d/2}$.  The leading nonzero coefficient has magnitude at least
one, and the Cauchy root bound therefore places every complex root in
$|z|\le1+d^{d/2}<B$.  Hence $P(B)\ne0$, contradicting
$v(B)\in\operatorname{col}(A_\sigma)$.  Thus
$\operatorname{aux}_k(f)>\ell$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:zero-temperature} finishes the proof.
\end{proof}
