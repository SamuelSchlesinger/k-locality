# Circuit and exact-recognition consequences

## Executive verdict

The fixed-positive-budget recognition route does **not** currently support an
`exists-R`-hardness claim.  In fact, the dense-table input convention makes
such a claim conditionally implausible: exact recognition with fixed locality
and any linear latent budget has a deterministic quasipolynomial-time
algorithm.  In particular, for `k=2` and `ell=1`, a table of bit length `L` can
be recognized in time

```text
exp(O(log^3 L)).
```

For the full-support tables emphasized in the paper, the one-bit bound
improves to `exp(O(log^2 L))`.  More generally, fixed-`ell` full-support
recognition at locality `k` takes `exp(O_{k,ell}(log^k L))` time.  This sharper
bound removes all purely visible energy and Gibbs parameters before invoking
real algebraic decision procedures.

Consequently, an NP-hardness reduction of polynomial output size would refute
the Exponential Time Hypothesis.  Since `NP` is contained in `exists-R`, the
same applies a fortiori to `exists-R`-hardness.  This is an unconditional
algorithmic theorem plus a standard conditional barrier, not a proof that the
problem is in `P`.

The theorem is worth adding to the manuscript.  It materially strengthens the
current `exists-R` upper bound and closes off a tempting but poorly matched
flagship route.  It is not, by itself, a top-tier complexity flagship: the
remaining gap is between polynomial and quasipolynomial time on an explicitly
listed probability table.

The literature audit also identifies the exact one-hidden-bit algebraic
model.  On the positive stratum, a quadratic one-bit localization is the
Hadamard product of a visible Ising model and a nonnegative-rank-two binary
tensor.  The full localization model is the topological closure of this
positive product.  Known universality theorems for nonnegative factorization
use a target rank that grows with the input and therefore do not transfer.
Rank two itself has an explicit semialgebraic characterization.

## Precise decision problem

The input is a **dense exact rational table**

```text
D = (D(x) : x in {0,1}^n),
```

including zero entries, with each entry written as a binary numerator and
positive denominator.  Let `||D||_bit` denote its total bit length.  Given
`k >= 2` and `ell >= 0`, the question is whether `L_k(D) <= ell`, where the
joint law may lie anywhere in the topological closure of the order-`k`
hierarchical model on `N=n+ell` bits.

This encoding choice is decisive.  If `L` is the total input length, then
`L >= 2^n`.  Algorithms exponential or even mildly superexponential in `n`
can therefore still be polynomial or quasipolynomial in `L`.  Hardness results
for succinct graphical models, implicitly described marginal polytopes, or a
sample oracle address different decision problems.

## Main theorem: quasipolynomial dense recognition

Write

```text
N = n + ell,
M = 2^N,
d = d_k(N) = sum_{j=0}^k binom(N,j).
```

### Theorem (face-enumeration recognition)

There is a deterministic bit-model algorithm deciding whether
`L_k(D) <= ell` in time

```text
poly(||D||_bit + N) (M d)^{O(d)}.
```

Consequently, for every fixed `k` and constant `c`, the language of
well-formed dense tables satisfying

```text
ell <= c n  and  L_k(D) <= ell
```

is decidable in time

```text
L^{O_{k,c}(log^k L)}
  = exp(O_{k,c}(log^{k+1} L)),
```

where `L` is the total input length.  In particular, fixed `ell=1`, `k=2`
recognition is in `TIME(exp(O(log^3 L)))`.

### Proof

Let `chi_k:{0,1}^N -> {0,1}^d` be the degree-at-most-`k` feature map,
including the constant feature, and put

```text
P_k(N) = conv{chi_k(y) : y in {0,1}^N}.
```

Because the singleton features are present, the map is injective and every
`chi_k(y)` is a vertex: the affine Hamming-distance functional uniquely
minimizes at `y`.

The face--Gibbs theorem says that every order-`k` law in the closure has a
support `T` that is the inverse image of a nonempty face of `P_k(N)`, and that
on `T` its unnormalized weights have the form

```text
z_y(theta) = product_{empty != A subseteq [N], |A| <= k}
             theta_A^{y_A},                    theta_A > 0.       (1)
```

Conversely, every face and every positive choice of the parameters in (1)
give an admissible trace-family law.  This is the finite exponential-family
face stratification [rka11][rka11].

We first enumerate all possible supports, with repetition.  Every face `F` of
`P_k(N)` has an affine basis consisting of at most `d` of its vertices.  If
`B` is such a basis, then `F` is the smallest face containing `B`.  Hence it
suffices to enumerate all nonempty subsets `B` of at most `d` lifted states.
There are at most `M^{O(d)}` such subsets.

For completeness, the smallest face generated by a candidate `B` can be
recovered by rational linear programming.  A lifted state `y` is outside that
face if and only if the following system in `a in R^d` is feasible:

```text
<a,chi_k(b)> = 0       for b in B,
<a,chi_k(z)> >= 0      for every z in {0,1}^N,
<a,chi_k(y)> >= 1.                                      (2)
```

If `y` lies outside the smallest face, an exposing functional for that face
can be rescaled to satisfy (2).  Conversely, any solution of (2) exposes a
face containing `B` and excluding `y`.  Thus `M` polynomial-size rational LPs
recover the vertex set `T_B` of the smallest face.  This also handles the full
polytope face.

Fix one recovered face `T` and let `S=supp(D)`.  Reject this face unless its
visible projection is exactly `S`; positivity of the trace weights makes this
condition necessary.  Choose `x_0 in S`, and write `D(x)=p_x/r_x` with
`r_x>0`.  For each visible state define the polynomial

```text
S_x(theta) = sum_{h : (x,h) in T} z_(x,h)(theta).
```

The normalized trace law on `T` has visible marginal `D` if and only if the
following existential system is feasible:

```text
theta_A > 0                                                   (3)

p_(x_0) r_x S_x(theta) - p_x r_(x_0) S_(x_0)(theta) = 0
                                      for every x in S.       (4)
```

Indeed, (4) says that all positive numbers `S_x(theta)` have the same ratios
as the entries of `D`.  Since both normalized vectors sum to one, their
normalizations agree exactly.  No partition-function variable is needed.

System (3)--(4) uses `d-1` real variables, at most `2^n+d`
polynomials, and degree at most `d-1`.  Across all equations its sparse
encoding has `O(Md)` monomial occurrences, and its integer coefficient bit
length is polynomial in `||D||_bit`.  Singly exponential algorithms for the
existential theory of the reals decide such a system in

```text
(M d)^{O(d)} poly(||D||_bit)
```

bit operations [basu06][basu06] [renegar92][renegar92].  Multiplying by the
`M^{O(d)}` candidate faces and their polynomial-time LP work preserves the
same asymptotic form.

Soundness follows because a satisfying system constructs a trace Gibbs law on
a facial support with marginal `D`.  Completeness follows by taking the actual
support face of any `ell`-bit localization.  A localization using fewer bits
may be padded with hidden bits fixed to zero, so deciding exactly `ell` lifted
coordinates decides the inequality `L_k(D) <= ell`.

Finally, for fixed `k,c` and `ell<=cn`, the dense encoding gives

```text
M = 2^{n+ell} <= L^{c+1},
N <= (c+1) log_2 L,
d = O_{k,c}(log^k L).
```

Substitution yields the claimed quasipolynomial bound.  QED.

### Why this is stronger than the current `exists-R` formula

The manuscript's direct formula introduces one or more variables for every
lifted state, so generic real-algebraic decision procedures see `Theta(M)`
variables.  The proof above first fixes a facial stratum.  On a fixed face,
all state weights are monomials in only `d-1` Gibbs parameters.  The number of
faces is at most `M^{O(d)}`, exactly the amount that can be absorbed into the
same quasipolynomial bound.

The improvement is therefore not just a citation to a faster ETR solver; it
uses the paper's face geometry to reduce the number of continuous variables
from exponential to polylogarithmic in the dense input length.

## Sharper theorem for full support and fixed latent budget

Full support permits a second reduction in the number of continuous
variables.  It is exactly the setting in which the support face cannot carry
an arbitrary purely visible offset: that offset is determined pointwise by
the hidden-dependent part of the exposing energy.

For fixed `k` and `ell`, put

```text
r = d_k(n+ell) - d_k(n).
```

This is the number of degree-at-most-`k` monomials involving at least one
hidden coordinate.  For fixed positive `ell`,

```text
r = O_{k,ell}(n^{k-1}).
```

### Theorem (few-variable full-support recognition)

Let `D` be a strictly positive dense rational table on `{0,1}^n`.  For fixed
`k` and `ell`, whether `L_k(D)<=ell` can be decided deterministically in time

```text
L^{O_{k,ell}(log^{k-1} L)}
  = exp(O_{k,ell}(log^k L)).
```

For `k=2` and `ell=1`, this is `exp(O(log^2 L))`.

### Proof

Separate every canonical degree-`k` polynomial on visible and hidden bits
into its purely visible and hidden-dependent terms.  Write an exposing energy
and a trace Gibbs potential as

```text
E(x,h) = A(x) + B(x,h),
G(x,h) = C(x) + J(x,h),                              (5)
```

where every monomial of `B` and `J` contains a hidden coordinate.  In
particular, `B(x,0^ell)=J(x,0^ell)=0`.  Each of `B,J` has `r` coefficients.

Because `D` has full support, the zero set of `E` has a nonempty fiber above
every `x`.  Nonnegativity of `E` therefore forces

```text
A(x) = - min_h B(x,h),
H_x  = argmin_h B(x,h),                              (6)
```

where `H_x` is the lifted support fiber.  Conversely, if the function on the
right of (6) has Boolean degree at most `k`, then (5)--(6) define a
nonnegative degree-`k` exposing energy.  Thus the `d_k(n)` coefficients of
`A` need not be existentially quantified.

Exponentiate the coefficients of `J`.  For each hidden-dependent monomial
`R`, introduce a positive variable `u_R`, and define

```text
W_h(x;u) = product_R u_R^{(x,h)_R},
R_{B,u}(x) = sum_{h in H_x} W_h(x;u).                 (7)
```

The visible marginal of the trace Gibbs law is `D` exactly when one can
choose `B,u` so that

```text
-min_h B(x,h)                 has degree at most k,
log D(x) - log R_{B,u}(x)     has degree at most k.    (8)
```

Indeed, in that case set `A=-min B` and
`C(x)=log D(x)-log R_{B,u}(x)`.  On the exposed support, assign weight
`exp(C(x)+J(x,h))`.  Summing over `h in H_x` gives exactly `D(x)`, hence the
joint weights already sum to one.  Conversely, every full-support
localization has decompositions (5), satisfies (6), and yields (7)--(8) after
absorbing its normalizing constant into the constant term of `C`.

It remains to encode (8) with only the `2r` variables consisting of the
coefficients of `B` and the positive `u_R`.  A real function on the Boolean
cube has degree at most `k` if and only if its alternating sum vanishes on
every `(k+1)`-dimensional subcube.  Fix such a subcube `Q`.  Since `ell` is
fixed, there are constantly many nonempty candidate minimizer sets
`K subseteq {0,1}^ell`.  The assertion `H_x=K` is the conjunction

```text
B(x,h) = B(x,h_0)   for h in K,
B(x,g) > B(x,h_0)   for g not in K,                  (9)
```

for any fixed `h_0 in K`.  For every tuple `(K_x:x in Q)`, use (9) and
substitute

```text
-B(x,h_0)                         for -min_h B(x,h),
sum_{h in K_x} W_h(x;u)           for R_{B,u}(x).
```

The first condition in (8) becomes one linear alternating-sum equation.  For
the second, exponentiate its alternating-sum equation.  If `Q_+` and `Q_-`
are its even and odd vertices, respectively, the resulting polynomial
identity is

```text
product_{x in Q_+} D(x)  product_{x in Q_-} R_{B,u}(x)
 =
product_{x in Q_-} D(x)  product_{x in Q_+} R_{B,u}(x).   (10)
```

Clear the rational denominators in (10).  Take the disjunction over the
constantly many tuples of minimizer sets, and repeat for every `Q`.  The
strict comparisons in (9) handle exact boundary faces; ties correspond to
minimizer sets of cardinality greater than one.  Because the actual minimizer
set for a fixed `B` is unique, independently chosen disjuncts on overlapping
subcubes are automatically consistent.

There are `O_k(2^n n^{k+1})` relevant subcubes.  The resulting quantifier-free
formula has polynomial size in `L`, `2r` real variables, and polynomial degree
`O_{k,ell}(r)`.  Singly exponential real-algebraic decision therefore takes
`L^{O_{k,ell}(r)}` time.  Since `L>=2^n` and
`r=O_{k,ell}(n^{k-1})`, this is the claimed
`L^{O_{k,ell}(log^{k-1}L)}` bound.  As in the general theorem, a localization
using fewer than `ell` hidden bits can be padded by hidden coordinates exposed
at zero, so the formula for exactly `ell` hidden coordinates decides the
budget inequality.  QED.

The proof explains why full support is algorithmically useful even though it
destroys support-circuit lower bounds.  With zeros in `D`, an absent visible
fiber need only have strictly positive minimum energy; the purely visible
part `A(x)` is no longer determined by `B`, and the variable count returns to
the order of `d_k(n+ell)`.

The exact-arithmetic sanity check
[`data/validate_full_support_recognition_reduction.py`](data/validate_full_support_recognition_reduction.py)
verifies the energy, fiber, marginal-factorization, and conditional-odds
identities on three representative quadratic one-bit faces.  It is a check of
the formulas, not a substitute for the proof.

## Complexity consequence: a hardness barrier

### Corollary (ETH barrier)

Fix `k` and `c`.  If the dense recognition language above is NP-hard under
polynomial-time many-one reductions, then the Exponential Time Hypothesis is
false.  The same holds for any fixed-budget restriction, including `k=2,
ell=1`.  In particular, `exists-R`-hardness under polynomial-time reductions
would refute ETH.

### Proof

Compose a hypothetical polynomial-output reduction from sparse `3`-SAT with
the theorem.  An instance of size `m` produces a table of length
`L=m^{O(1)}`, and the recognition algorithm runs in

```text
exp(O(log^{k+1} L)) = exp(polylog(m)) = 2^{o(m)}.
```

The sparsification formulation of ETH excludes such an algorithm
[ipz01][ipz01].  Finally, `NP subseteq exists-R` by the elementary encoding of
a Boolean variable as `x(1-x)=0`, so `exists-R`-hardness implies NP-hardness.
QED.

This corollary does **not** rule out NP-hardness unconditionally, nor does it
apply to succinctly represented probability laws.  It says that fixed-budget
dense recognition is the wrong target for a conventional hardness flagship
unless the reduction exploits a nonstandard encoding or simultaneously
breaks a central fine-grained conjecture.

## A polynomial-time stratum: graph-supported lifts

The remaining real-algebraic difficulty can be localized more sharply.

### Proposition (a specified promised facial graph trace is in `P`)

Suppose `T subseteq {0,1}^{n+ell}` is supplied explicitly with the promise that
it is facial, and every visible fiber of `T` has size at most one.  Given a
dense rational table `D`, one can decide in deterministic polynomial time
whether a trace order-`k` law on `T` has visible marginal `D`.

### Proof

First check that the projection of `T` is `supp(D)`.  Since `T` is a graph over
that support, the only possible lifted law is

```text
q(x,h(x)) = D(x).
```

Let `A_T` be the matrix whose columns are `chi_k(y)`, `y in T`.  The trace
Gibbs condition is

```text
log(q|_T) in rowspan_R(A_T).
```

Compute an integer basis of `ker(A_T)` by fraction-free elimination.  For
each basis vector `z`, the condition is exactly the positive-integer
power-product equality

```text
product_{y in T} q(y)^{z_y^+}
  = product_{y in T} q(y)^{z_y^-}.
```

After clearing the rational numerators and denominators, these equalities are
decidable in polynomial time by gcd refinement, without integer
factorization [etessami14][etessami14].  QED.

Thus the continuous trace-family test is easy once a facial graph support is
specified.  Global recognition could still be hard because it must choose
among graph faces, so this proposition does not prove that every source of
hardness must use genuine latent mixing.

## Exact algebraic dictionary for `k=2`, one hidden bit

On the positive joint stratum, write the quadratic log-weight as

```text
g(x,h) = A(x) + h B(x),
deg(A) <= 2,
deg(B) <= 1.
```

Marginalizing `h` gives

```text
D(x) proportional to exp(A(x)) (1 + exp(B(x))).          (11)
```

The first factor is a positive visible Ising-model tensor.  Because `B` is
affine, the second factor is the sum of two strictly positive rank-one binary
tensors.  Conversely, if `I` is a positive pairwise Ising tensor and

```text
R = a_1 tensor ... tensor a_n + b_1 tensor ... tensor b_n
```

with all one-coordinate factors strictly positive, then one may factor the
first rank-one summand into the unary part of `I`; the remaining ratio has the
form `1+exp(B(x))` for an affine `B`.  Therefore

```text
marginals of strictly positive joint quadratic laws with one hidden bit
  = normalize(Ising_2^+ Hadamard-product RankPlus_2^+).   (12)
```

Since the closed joint order-two model is the closure of its positive
exponential family and is compact inside the probability simplex, its
continuous marginal image is compact.  It follows in both directions that the
complete one-bit localization model is the topological closure of the set in
(12).  The closure qualification is essential: a full-support visible table
may be approached through joint laws whose limiting joint support is
defective.

For an RBM with one hidden unit and no visible--visible interactions, the
Ising factor in (12) has only unary terms and can be absorbed into the rank-two
tensor.  This recovers the standard identification of the one-hidden-unit RBM
with the first secant of the Segre variety
[cuetomortonsturmfels10][cuetomortonsturmfels10].  The general localization
model is larger because the Ising factor includes arbitrary visible pair
interactions.

This gives a concrete residual decision problem:

> **Pairwise-gauged rank-two closure.** Given a positive rational binary
> tensor `D`, decide whether `D` lies in the closure of the normalized
> Hadamard products `I Hadamard-product R`, where `I` is a positive pairwise
> Ising tensor and `R` is a sum of two positive rank-one tensors.

Solving this problem in polynomial time would settle the strictly positive
joint stratum.  A polynomial-time algorithm for the complete one-bit model
would additionally require a uniform treatment of boundary strata.  The
face-enumeration theorem supplies a quasipolynomial-time solution but not a
polynomial one.

## Why the apparent hardness sources do not transfer

### Nonnegative matrix factorization

Shitov's universality theorem realizes arbitrary bounded semialgebraic sets as
spaces of nonnegative factorizations, and therefore gives `exists-R` hardness
for general nonnegative rank [shitov16][shitov16].  The constructed target
rank grows with the encoded semialgebraic instance.  Moitra, by contrast,
decides matrix nonnegative rank at most `r` in time `(mn)^{O(r^2)}`; every
fixed `r`, in particular `r=2`, is polynomial-time
[moitra16][moitra16].

One hidden bit supplies two mixture components only in the restricted
conditional-independence submodel.  It does not supply the variable rank used
by universality.  Encoding a rank-`r` mixture with one-hot hidden indicators
would cost `Theta(r)` latent bits, not one bit, and encoding arbitrary rows and
columns as Boolean visible strings while retaining a dense full table would
make the reduction output exponentially large.

### Nonnegative tensor rank and latent classes

General tensor-rank problems are NP-hard [hillarlim13][hillarlim13], but the
rank-two nonnegative latent-class model is exceptional.  Allman, Rhodes,
Sturmfels, and Zwiernik prove that a nonnegative tensor has nonnegative rank at
most two exactly when it has flattening rank at most two and is supermodular
after suitable per-mode permutations [allman15][allman15].  For a dense
binary tensor the number of mode permutations, flattenings, minors, and
supermodular inequalities is polynomial in the table length, yielding a
polynomial-time membership test.

The new issue in (12) is not rank two itself but existential **pairwise
Hadamard gauging**, plus topological boundary strata.  None of the audited
rank-hardness reductions preserves that fixed-rank, Boolean-mode, dense-table
form.

### Marginal-polytope hardness

Membership in correlation and marginal polytopes is hard when the polytope is
given succinctly and its `2^n` vertices are implicit; Pitowsky's correlation
polytope theorem is the canonical example [pitowsky91][pitowsky91].  Here the
input already lists `2^n` probabilities.  Listing all feature vertices is
polynomial in the dense input length, and the LPs in (2) are therefore
polynomial-size.  The remaining nonlinear trace-family test has only
`d_k(N)-1=polylog(L)` continuous variables, which is precisely what yields the
quasipolynomial algorithm.

### Marginal ideals and exponential varieties

Toric and secant ideals describe the complex Zariski closure of a positive
model [geiger06][geiger06] [cuetomortonsturmfels10][cuetomortonsturmfels10].
The theory of exponential varieties develops the associated algebraic and
convex geometry [michalek16][michalek16].  These equations are useful
necessary certificates, but complex-variety membership alone does not impose
nonnegativity, select the correct semialgebraic component, or distinguish the
facial support strata required by exact localization.  The face enumeration
above is the missing real-boundary step.  The audited sources do not state a
matching hardness result for this exact dense rational closure problem.

## Circuit compilation audit

No stronger standard-circuit theorem emerged from the recognition route.  A
degree-`k` exposing energy still gives the exact support normal form

```text
OR_{h in {0,1}^ell} o EQTHR o AND_{<=k},
```

with the slice coefficients coupled by one jointly degree-`k` polynomial.  At
`ell=1` this is an `OR_2` of two exact-threshold tests on the visible
degree-at-most-`k` monomials.  Replacing real exact-threshold weights by bounded
integers and simulating addition gives the manuscript's polynomial-size
fan-in-two circuit.  The quasipolynomial recognition algorithm decides a
language whose input is the entire probability table; it does not improve the
circuit size of the visible support predicate.

Likewise, the Hadamard-product identity (12) is a statistical-model
factorization, not a Boolean circuit upper bound.  Full-support distributions
continue to have the constant-one support circuit, so weight-sensitive
recognition cannot transfer to a nontrivial support lower bound.

## Recommended manuscript integration

1. Add the face-enumeration theorem immediately after the existing
   `exists-R` membership theorem.  The proof is self-contained once the
   face--Gibbs theorem and a singly exponential real-algebraic decision bound
   are available.
2. Follow it with the few-variable full-support theorem.  Its proof should be
   stated in the main text through the identities `A=-min B` and
   `C=log D-log R`, with the subcube disjunction and complexity bookkeeping in
   an appendix.
3. Replace the open request for `exists-R`-hardness at `ell=1` by the sharper
   question of whether dense fixed-budget recognition is in `P`.  State the
   ETH barrier to NP-hardness.
4. Include the promised-facial graph proposition only as a diagnostic remark:
   it shows that the continuous trace test is easy after such a face is
   supplied, without claiming that choosing among graph faces is easy.
5. Present (12) as the exact connection to latent-class and RBM geometry, with
   the closure caveat.  Do not cite variable-rank NMF universality as evidence
   for fixed-one-bit hardness.
6. Add one compact flow diagram for the general algorithm: dense table and
   budget; enumeration of at most `d` lifted vertices; recovery of the smallest
   face by rational LP; `d-1` trace-Gibbs coordinates; real feasibility of the
   marginal-ratio equations; yes/no recognition.  Bracket the middle stages as
   “discrete facial search” and “continuous trace search.”  The visual point is
   that there is no independent variable for each lifted state.
7. Do not promote this route as the paper's flagship complexity result.  Its
   honest value is a clean quasipolynomial algorithm and a hardness barrier.
   A top-tier flagship still needs a natural explicit localization lower
   bound, an explicit selector obstruction, or a genuinely new circuit
   consequence.

## Admission decision

**Admit as a secondary theorem; reject as the flagship route.**  The
face-enumeration theorem and its full-support sharpening are manuscript-ready
and genuinely improve the algorithmic state of the paper.  Their proofs use
the central facial stratification rather than generic black-box ETR
membership, so they reinforce the paper's conceptual unity.  The conditional
consequence also prevents the paper from advertising an implausible
fixed-budget hardness problem.

The unresolved polynomial-time question is concrete and technically honest:
can pairwise-gauged nonnegative rank two, including its boundary closures, be
recognized in polynomial time from a dense rational tensor?  Answering that
would finish this route, but even a `P` theorem would remain more naturally an
algebraic-statistics result than a circuit-lower-bound flagship.
